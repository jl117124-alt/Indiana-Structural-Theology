# **SET V — SUBJECT CONDITION AND FINAL ADJUDICATION**

## ***(Canonical Form — Constraint Completion Layer)***

---

## **I. PURPOSE**

To determine:

**What internal condition within a subject must exist such that final adjudication is both necessary and non-arbitrary, given all prior constraints (Sets I–IV)**

---

## **BOUNDARY**

This set assumes:

* death removes embodied agency (Set I)  
* identity persists beyond death (Set I)  
* identity requires a singular, active, self-existent ground (Set II)  
* identity–existence convergence is established (Set II)  
* mediation between ground and subjects is required (Set IV)

---

This set does NOT:

* identify the mediating subject  
* introduce theological terminology  
* alter prior derivations  
* assume moral frameworks

---

It establishes only:

**The condition in subjects that determines outcome under final adjudication**

---

---

# **II. POST-CONVERGENCE PROBLEM**

From Sets I–IV:

* multiple subjects exist  
* all undergo death  
* all require restoration  
* one mediating subject resolves death

---

## **Constraint**

If all subjects:

* persist in identity  
* require restoration

---

Then:

**Final outcomes cannot be identical for all subjects without collapsing adjudication**

---

## **Result**

**A differentiating condition in subjects is required**

---

---

# **III. REQUIREMENT OF DIFFERENTIATION**

The differentiating condition must be:

---

### **R1 — Subject-Real**

It must exist **in the subject**, not externally assigned.

---

### **R2 — Pre-Death Formed**

It must exist **before death**, since post-death formation is impossible.

---

### **R3 — Death-Transcending**

It must persist through death.

---

### **R4 — Identity-Relevant**

It must affect restoration outcome.

---

---

# **IV. ELIMINATION OF CANDIDATES**

---

## **Option 1 — Behavior**

* ceases at death  
* not present at adjudication

→ **Fails R3**

---

## **Option 2 — Structure**

* persists without identity  
* insufficient for subject restoration

→ **Fails R4**

---

## **Option 3 — Memory**

* transferable  
* duplicable

→ **Fails identity constraint (Set I / II)**

---

## **Option 4 — External Assignment**

* not internal to subject

→ **Fails R1**

---

---

## **Elimination Result**

**No system-bound property satisfies all constraints**

---

---

# **V. FORCED CATEGORY**

Remaining possibility:

**A condition grounded in the subject’s basis of life**

---

---

# **VI. TWO LIFE CLASSES (DERIVED)**

---

## **VI.1 System-Bound Life**

* originates within system  
* dependent on embodiment  
* terminates at death

---

## **VI.2 Non-Perishable Life**

* not system-originated  
* persists through death  
* capable of sustaining restored embodiment

---

---

## **Result**

**Only non-perishable life satisfies R2–R4**

---

---

# **VII. ORIGIN CONSTRAINT**

---

## **Test — Self-Origin**

Subject cannot generate non-perishable life:

* lacks post-death agency  
* lacks pre-death capacity for self-originated permanence

→ **Eliminated**

---

## **Test — System-Origin**

System produces only system-bound life.

→ **Eliminated**

---

---

## **Forced Result**

**Non-perishable life must originate from the identity–existence ground**

---

---

# **VIII. MEDIATION CONSTRAINT**

From Set IV:

* restoration passes through a mediating subject

---

## **Test — Direct Ground-to-Subject Transfer**

If life is given without mediation:

* mediator becomes structurally unnecessary

→ **Contradiction**

---

---

## **Result**

**Non-perishable life must be mediated through the death-resolving subject**

---

---

# **IX. FORMATION REQUIREMENT**

---

## **Test — External Attribution**

If life is external only:

* subject remains unchanged

→ fails differentiation

---

## **Test — Mechanical Transfer**

If life is inserted:

* subject remains passive container

→ fails subject-real condition

---

---

## **Result**

**Non-perishable life must be formed inwardly in the subject**

---

---

# **X. CONTINUITY REQUIREMENT**

---

## **Test — Static Reception**

If life is received once and held independently:

* subject becomes self-sustaining

→ violates ground dependence (Set II)

---

---

## **Result**

**Life must be continuously derived from the ground through the mediator**

---

---

# **XI. PARTICIPATION CONSTRAINT**

---

## **Given**

* life is real in subject  
* life is continuously derived

---

## **Test — No Participation**

If subject is bypassed:

* condition not real in subject

→ contradiction

---

## **Test — Self-Initiation**

If subject initiates:

* violates origin constraint

→ contradiction

---

---

## **Result**

**Participation is required but cannot be self-originated**

---

---

# **XII. ENABLEMENT REQUIREMENT**

---

## **Given**

Subject cannot initiate participation.

---

## **Test — No Enablement**

Then participation never occurs.

→ system stalls

---

## **Test — Selective Enablement**

Then differentiation is externally imposed.

→ adjudication collapses

---

---

## **Result**

**Enablement must be universally extended from the ground through the mediator**

---

---

# **XIII. POINT OF DIVERGENCE**

---

All subjects:

* receive enablement  
* are exposed to mediated life

---

Yet outcomes differ.

---

## **Constraint**

Difference must arise:

* within subject  
* without self-origin  
* without coercion

---

---

## **Forced Result**

**The difference is the subject’s stance toward its own origin**

---

---

# **XIV. SUBJECT STATES (EXHAUSTIVE)**

---

## **State A — Self-Origin Retention**

* subject operates as its own source  
* rejects continuous derivation

---

## **State B — Self-Origin Relinquishment**

* subject ceases self-sourcing  
* exists in derived dependence

---

---

## **Exhaustiveness**

No third category exists:

* either source is self  
* or source is ground

---

---

# **XV. NATURE OF RELINQUISHMENT**

---

## **Test — Action Production**

If relinquishment is produced:

* becomes self-origin act

→ contradiction

---

## **Test — External Imposition**

If imposed:

* violates subject integrity

→ contradiction

---

---

## **Result**

**Relinquishment is the cessation of self-maintenance as source under enablement**

---

---

# **XVI. TEMPORAL CONSTRAINT**

---

## **At Death**

* no agency  
* no formation possible

---

---

## **Result**

**Condition must be fully formed prior to death**

---

---

## **At Judgment**

* condition cannot change

---

---

## **Result**

**Judgment reveals, not creates, the condition**

---

---

# **XVII. FINAL ADJUDICATION STRUCTURE**

---

Adjudication must:

---

### **A — Identify the Subject**

* same one

---

### **B — Recognize Condition**

* self-origin retained  
* or derived-life present

---

### **C — Apply Outcome**

* restoration  
* or non-restoration

---

---

# **XVIII. INTEGRATION WITH PRIOR SETS**

---

### **Set I — Death**

* removes agency  
* fixes inability

---

### **Set II — Ground**

* source of identity and existence  
* personal-capable, self-existent

---

### **Set IV — Mediator**

* resolves death  
* carries life

---

### **Set V — This Set**

* determines subject condition  
* grounds final differentiation

---

# **XIX. FINAL CONSTRAINT CHAIN**

---

1. Death removes embodied agency  
2. Identity persists  
3. Restoration required  
4. Adjudication required  
5. Differentiation required  
6. Condition must be internal  
7. Condition must transcend death  
8. Condition must be non-self-originated  
9. Life must come from ground  
10. Life must be mediated  
11. Life must be formed in subject  
12. Participation required  
13. Enablement universal  
14. Divergence at self-origin vs derived-life  
15. Condition fixed before death  
16. Judgment reveals condition

---

---

# **XX. TERMINAL STATEMENT**

---

**If identity persists beyond death and restoration is required through a mediating subject, then a non-perishable life originating from the identity–existence ground must be formed within subjects prior to death, universally enabled but not self-originated, and final adjudication necessarily differentiates subjects based on whether they retain themselves as the source of their own life or exist in continuous derivation from that ground.**
