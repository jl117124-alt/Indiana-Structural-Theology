# **SET III — REVELATION CORRIDOR**

## **VOLUME III.5 — REVELATION AND IDENTITY RESOLUTION (TERMINAL FORM)**

*(Canonical Form — Corridor Completion)*

---

## **I. Purpose**

To establish:

**The exact condition under which identity becomes resolved once revelation is introduced under full constraint**

This document does not introduce identity.

It defines:

**when identity is considered resolved and when it is not**

---

## **II. Boundary**

This document assumes:

* identity is underdetermined by structure (Volume III.1)  
* revelation is necessary (Volume III.2)  
* valid revelation is constrained (Volume III.3)  
* revelation functions as an identity-carrying corridor (Volume III.4)

This document does NOT assume:

* that any specific revelation is valid  
* that identity has been resolved in practice  
* that any subject is identified

We are not naming the subject.

We are defining **the condition that must be met for identity to be considered resolved**.

---

## **III. Starting Condition**

We now have:

* a fully constrained solution structure (Sets I & II)  
* a non-derivable identity (III.1)  
* a necessary disclosure mechanism (III.2)  
* constrained revelation (III.3)  
* revelation as identity-carrying corridor (III.4)

The remaining question is:

**When does identity become resolved?**

---

## **IV. Definition — Identity Resolution**

Identity is resolved when:

**a single, determinate subject is disclosed through revelation and that subject satisfies all structural constraints without violation**

---

This definition includes:

* disclosure (revelation)  
* referent (subject)  
* constraint satisfaction (Sets I & II)  
* uniqueness (no competitors)

---

## **V. Conditions for Resolution**

Identity is resolved if and only if all of the following are satisfied simultaneously.

No partial satisfaction qualifies.

---

### **V.1 Structural Alignment**

#### **Condition**

The revealed subject must:

**satisfy all constraints derived from Set I and Set II**

---

#### **Includes**

* same-person identity restoration  
* continuity through death  
* embodiment  
* representational capability  
* identity grounded in the non-derived source

---

#### **Failure Condition**

If the subject:

* violates any constraint  
* fails any required condition

---

#### **Result**

**Identity not resolved**

---

### **V.2 Constraint Consistency**

#### **Condition**

The revelation must:

**not contradict any prior derivation**

---

#### **Meaning**

It must:

* preserve all established requirements  
* not introduce incompatible claims

---

#### **Failure Condition**

If revelation:

* conflicts with prior constraints  
* requires reinterpretation of established structure

---

#### **Result**

**Invalid**

---

### **V.3 Referential Determinacy**

#### **Condition**

The revelation must:

**identify a single, specific subject**

---

#### **Meaning**

It must not:

* describe a class  
* describe a type  
* remain abstract

It must:

* specify a determinate referent

---

#### **Failure Condition**

If multiple subjects:

* fit the revelation equally

---

#### **Result**

**Identity not resolved**

---

### **V.4 Exclusivity**

#### **Condition**

The revealed identity must:

**exclude all competing candidates**

---

#### **Meaning**

No alternative subject may:

* equally satisfy both structure and revelation

---

#### **Failure Condition**

If multiple viable candidates remain:

---

#### **Result**

**Underdetermination persists**

---

### **V.5 Identity Continuity**

#### **Condition**

The subject must remain:

**the same one across all required stages**

---

#### **Includes**

* before death  
* through death  
* after restoration

---

#### **Failure Condition**

If identity:

* changes  
* fragments  
* is replaced

---

#### **Result**

**Invalid**

---

### **V.6 Accessibility (Dual-Domain, Enforced)**

#### **Condition**

Revelation must:

**be present within the human domain while preserving identity authority from the ground**

---

#### **Meaning**

The revealed identity must:

* be accessible to agents within the domain  
* not be reducible to system-dependent form  
* not originate from within the system

---

#### **Failure Condition**

If revelation is:

* inaccessible → cannot resolve identity  
* internally generated → loses authority  
* reducible to pattern or abstraction → identity collapse

---

#### **Result**

**Non-functional or invalid**

---

### **V.7 Identity-Carrying Integrity**

#### **Condition**

Revelation must:

**deliver identity without reducing it to description**

---

#### **Meaning**

Revelation must:

* preserve referential identity  
* not rely on reconstruction  
* not substitute pattern for person

---

#### **Failure Condition**

If identity is:

* inferred from description  
* reconstructed from information  
* treated as transferable pattern

---

#### **Result**

**Invalid**

---

### **V.8 Bridge Satisfaction (Set IV Compatibility)**

#### **Condition**

The revealed subject must:

**satisfy the cross-domain bridge required for solution execution**

---

#### **Meaning**

The subject must:

* originate from the identity ground  
* operate within the human domain  
* traverse death  
* preserve identity through rupture  
* reinstantiate embodiment

---

#### **Failure Condition**

If the subject:

* remains external  
* fails to enter the domain  
* does not traverse death  
* cannot reinstantiate identity

---

#### **Result**

**Eliminated**

---

## **VI. Resolution Mechanism**

Identity resolution occurs through:

**constrained matching between revealed identity and fully specified structural requirements**

---

This is not:

* inference  
* probability  
* interpretation

It is:

**direct correspondence under constraint**

---

## **VII. Failure Conditions (Consolidated)**

Identity is NOT resolved if any of the following occur:

---

### **VII.1 Multiple Candidates Remain**

→ underdetermination persists

---

### **VII.2 Structural Misalignment**

→ constraint violation

---

### **VII.3 Ambiguity**

→ no determinate referent

---

### **VII.4 Identity Discontinuity**

→ same-person requirement fails

---

### **VII.5 Reduction to Pattern or Description**

→ identity collapse

---

### **VII.6 Bridge Failure**

→ cross-domain requirement not satisfied

---

## **VIII. Result**

When all conditions are satisfied:

**identity becomes uniquely determined**

---

At that point:

* structure is complete  
* revelation is valid  
* identity is resolved

No further derivation is required.

No alternative candidates remain.

---

## **IX. Final Role of Revelation**

Revelation completes what structure cannot.

---

### **Structure provides:**

* necessity  
* constraints  
* required conditions

---

### **Revelation provides:**

* identity

---

### **Combined Result:**

**A fully specified, constraint-satisfying solution**

---

## **X. Locked Statement**

**Identity is resolved when a revealed subject uniquely satisfies all structural constraints, preserves identity without reduction, fulfills the cross-domain bridge, and excludes all competing candidates**

---

## **XI. Boundary Statement**

We are not saying who this is.

We are not saying that this has happened.

We are not pointing to any example.

We are doing something simpler and harder:

We are defining the finish line.

If something claims to reveal the identity, this is what it has to clear.

It has to hold together all the way through—without breaking identity, without violating the structure, without leaving loose ends.

If it does, identity is resolved.

If it doesn’t, it’s not.
