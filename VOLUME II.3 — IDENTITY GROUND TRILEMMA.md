# **SET II — CREATION CORRIDOR**

## **VOLUME II.3 — IDENTITY GROUND TRILEMMA**

*(Canonical Form — Refactored from Trilemma Layer)*

---

## **I. Purpose**

To determine:

**What type of ground is capable of sustaining same-one identity across change, representation, and rupture**

---

## **Boundary**

This document assumes:

* identity persistence is required  
* invariant scalar structure is established (Volume II.2)  
* identity is not reducible to system-state

This document does NOT assume:

* existence-ground convergence  
* self-existence  
* system-entry  
* any theological identification

---

---

## **II. Problem Definition**

Identity must:

* persist as the same one  
* remain invariant under valid re-description  
* survive transformation  
* remain non-duplicated  
* remain globally coherent

---

## **Additional Constraint**

Under rupture conditions:

* multiple structurally valid continuations may exist  
* identity must still resolve as **this one**

---

---

## **III. Candidate Ground Types**

All possible identity-ground types reduce to three classes:

---

### **Option 1 — Passive / Impersonal Ground**

Identity is grounded by:

* a non-active principle  
* a relation or property  
* a non-adjudicative structure

---

---

### **Option 2 — Distributed / Plural Ground**

Identity is grounded across:

* multiple local centers  
* domain-specific assignments  
* plural coordinating sources

---

---

### **Option 3 — Singular Active Ground**

Identity is grounded in:

* one source  
* capable of determination and continuity  
* capable of adjudication

---

---

## **IV. Evaluation — Option 1 (Passive / Impersonal)**

---

### **Properties:**

* non-adjudicative  
* non-selective  
* non-distinguishing

---

### **Test — Rupture**

Under rupture:

* multiple candidates for continuation may exist

---

### **Requirement:**

Identity must resolve as:

**this one**

---

### **Failure:**

Passive ground cannot:

* distinguish between candidates  
* adjudicate rightful continuity  
* exclude alternatives

---

---

### **Test — Non-Duplication**

Identity requires:

* exclusion of multiple simultaneous claimants

---

### **Failure:**

Passive structure cannot enforce exclusion

---

---

### **Test — Restoration**

If identity must be restored:

* restoration requires active determination

---

### **Failure:**

Passive ground cannot instantiate or restore identity

---

---

## **Result**

**Passive / impersonal ground is insufficient**

---

---

## **V. Evaluation — Option 2 (Distributed / Plural)**

---

### **Properties:**

* multiple identity sources  
* local grounding  
* distributed assignment

---

---

### **Test — Global Coherence**

Systems interact:

* composition occurs  
* cross-system identity must remain consistent

---

### **Failure:**

Distributed ground cannot guarantee:

* global identity consistency  
* cross-domain agreement

---

---

### **Test — Composition**

Composite systems require:

* unified identity across components

---

### **Failure:**

Multiple grounds require coordination

---

If coordination exists:

higher unifying ground is required

---

---

### **Test — Representation Independence**

Identity must remain invariant across:

* different descriptions  
* different partitions

---

### **Failure:**

Multiple grounds introduce:

* dependence on assignment  
* breakdown under transformation

---

---

## **Result**

**Distributed / plural ground is insufficient**

---

---

## **VI. Evaluation — Option 3 (Singular Active)**

---

### **Properties:**

* single source  
* capable of identification  
* capable of distinction  
* capable of continuity

---

---

### **Test — Rupture**

* identify rightful continuation  
* exclude alternatives

✔ satisfied

---

---

### **Test — Non-Duplication**

Can:

* enforce single identity assignment

✔ satisfied

---

---

### **Test — Restoration**

* re-identify same one  
* sustain continuity

✔ satisfied

---

---

### **Test — Global Coherence**

Can:

* maintain identity across all systems

✔ satisfied

---

---

## **Result**

**Singular active ground satisfies all constraints**

---

---

## **VII. Elimination Result**

---

All alternatives fail except one:

**Identity requires a singular active ground**

---

* singular  
* active  
* capable of identification

* capable of continuity  
* capable of adjudication
