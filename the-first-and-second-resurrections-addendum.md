# DOCTRINAL ADDENDUM
The First and Second Resurrections
(Clarificatory Insert for Volume VI — The Doctrine of the Millennium and Competing
Eschatological Systems)


1. Problem Stated
Revelation 20 introduces the phrase “first resurrection.”
The broader canonical teaching of Scripture, however, presents the resurrection of the dead as
the decisive transition between the intermediate state and final judgment.

Volume VI has already established the eschatological corridor:

Life → Death → Intermediate State → Resurrection → Judgment → Final State

This corridor reflects the consistent teaching of Scripture concerning the end of human history.

The interpretive question therefore arises:

How can Revelation 20 speak of a “first resurrection” without introducing multiple bodily
resurrections that fragment the eschatological corridor?

The answer is that Revelation 20 does not describe two bodily resurrections.

The first resurrection is the Spirit-wrought participation of the believer in the life of the
risen Christ through union with Him.

The second resurrection is the bodily resurrection of humanity at the return of Christ, which
remains the single climactic resurrection event of the eschatological corridor.

This distinction preserves both:

- the present resurrection language of John 5
- the future bodily resurrection language of John 5

without contradiction.
2. Definitions (Precise and Technical)
First Resurrection

The first resurrection is the Spirit-wrought participation of the elect in the risen life of Christ
through union with Him.

It is regeneration described under resurrectional categories.

It is not the restoration of the body from the grave.

It is the transition from spiritual death into covenantal life under the reign of Christ.


Second Resurrection

The second resurrection is the bodily resurrection of humanity at the return of Christ.

It restores embodied human life and prepares all persons for final judgment.

This is the resurrection that occupies the fixed place in the eschatological corridor between the
intermediate state and final judgment.


Second Death

The second death is the final judicial state from which there is no recovery.

Those who share in the first resurrection are secured against the second death because union
with Christ places them within His life prior to the final judgment horizon.


Intermediate State

The intermediate state is the conscious but incomplete condition of the human person after
death and prior to bodily resurrection.

It must not be confused with either:

- regeneration
- the final state
3. Governing Distinctions
The coherence of this doctrine requires the preservation of the following distinctions:

    1. Regeneration ≠ Bodily Resurrection
    2. Present Participation in Christ’s Life ≠ Final Restoration from the Grave
    3. Intermediate State ≠ Final State
    4. Spiritual Death ≠ Biological Death
    5. Symbol ≠ Mechanism
    6. Visionary Sequence ≠ Historical Chronology
    7. First Resurrection ≠ Second Resurrection
    8. Regeneration Language ≠ Final Resurrection Event

These distinctions ensure that the resurrection of the dead remains the decisive bodily transition
between the intermediate state and final judgment.


4. Redemptive Sequence
#### I. Christ the Firstborn from the Dead

Christ’s resurrection is the prototype and ground of all resurrection life.

All later resurrection realities derive from Him.


#### II. Present Application Through the Spirit

In the present age, the Spirit unites the elect to Christ and grants participation in His life.

This is the present-tense resurrectional reality described by Christ when He declares:

      “The hour is coming, and now is, when the dead will hear the voice of the Son of
      God, and those who hear will live.”

This resurrection life belongs to the present redemptive age.
#### III. The Church Age as the Millennial Horizon

Volume VI has already established that the millennium corresponds to the present reign of
Christ during the church age.

Therefore the first resurrection belongs to the present age rather than to the final return of
Christ.


#### IV. Death and the Intermediate State

Believers who die do not yet enter bodily resurrection.

They enter the intermediate state and await the restoration of embodied life.


#### V. The Return of Christ and the Bodily Resurrection of the Dead

At the return of Christ, all who are in the tombs hear His voice and come forth.

This is the bodily resurrection that restores embodied life and prepares humanity for judgment.

This resurrection remains singular in its climactic role.


#### VI. Final Judgment and Final State

Following the resurrection of the dead comes the final judgment.

After judgment the final state begins.

No additional historical kingdom intervenes between resurrection and final consummation.


5. Mechanism Explained Step-by-Step
Step 1 — Christ Rises as the Prototype

Resurrection life originates in Christ.
He is the living source from whom all resurrection realities proceed.


Step 2 — The Spirit Unites the Elect to Christ

In the present age the Spirit joins the elect to the risen Christ.

Through this union believers pass from death into life.


Step 3 — This Transition Is Called the First Resurrection

Because the believer is raised from spiritual death into covenantal life and reign with Christ,
Revelation 20 refers to this participation as the first resurrection.

The term “first” marks priority of redemptive order, not the first event in a chain of bodily
resurrections.


Step 4 — Physical Death Still Occurs

Participation in the first resurrection does not eliminate biological death.

Believers still die and enter the intermediate state.


Step 5 — The Second Resurrection Is Bodily

At Christ’s return the dead are raised bodily.

This resurrection functions as the pivot between the intermediate state and final judgment.


Step 6 — The Second Death Has No Power

Those who have already been brought into resurrection life through union with Christ are secure
against final condemnation.
6. Textual Reconciliation
John 5:25 and John 5:28–29

Christ distinguishes two horizons.

First:

         “The hour is coming, and now is, when the dead will hear the voice of the Son of
         God and live.”

Second:

         “The hour is coming when all who are in the tombs will hear His voice.”

The first refers to present spiritual life.
The second refers to future bodily resurrection.


Revelation 20:4–6

The first resurrection describes the present participation of believers in the life and reign of
Christ.

The final bodily resurrection occurs at the return of Christ.


1 Corinthians 15:23–26

Paul identifies the resurrection at the coming of Christ as the event that introduces the final
defeat of death.

This is the bodily resurrection that concludes the present age.


Daniel 12:2

Daniel describes the awakening of the dead leading directly into final destinies.

This again confirms the resurrection as the decisive transition into final judgment.
7. Structural Integrity Checks
This clarification preserves:

- the unity of bodily resurrection
- the unity of final judgment
- the integrity of the eschatological corridor
- the distinction between regeneration and glorification
- the distinction between intermediate and final states
- the symbolic discipline required by apocalyptic literature


8. Objection Handling
Objection 1

Does this make resurrection symbolic?

No.
The bodily resurrection remains fully literal and occupies the fixed place within the corridor.


Objection 2

Does this introduce two unrelated resurrections?

No.

They are related by source but distinguished by kind:

- regeneration participates in resurrection life
- the final resurrection restores the body


Objection 3

Does this confuse regeneration with the intermediate state?

No.
Regeneration occurs during earthly life.

The intermediate state occurs after biological death.


Objection 4

Why call regeneration resurrection?

Because Christ Himself uses resurrection language for the granting of spiritual life prior to the
final bodily resurrection.


9. Final Doctrinal Thesis
The first resurrection of Revelation 20 is the Spirit-wrought participation of the elect in the
risen life of Christ through union with Him, that is, regeneration described under resurrectional
categories.

It is therefore distinct from the bodily resurrection of the dead at the return of Christ, which
alone occupies the fixed place of resurrection within the eschatological corridor between the
intermediate state and final judgment.

By preserving this distinction, the doctrine:

- eliminates the need for multiple bodily resurrections
- secures the unity of resurrection and final judgment
- preserves the integrity of the eschatological corridor
- explains why those who share in the first resurrection are beyond the power of the second
death.
