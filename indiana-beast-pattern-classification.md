This document memorializes a constraint‑based classification system developed through

1. Purpose
To provide a reusable classification system for analyzing large‑scale civilizational
coordination patterns using the Indiana framework, specifically:

   - Beast
   - Woman
   - False Prophet

These are treated strictly as classes of function, not identities, actors, peoples, or prophetic
fulfillments.


2. Core Guardrails (Locked)
   -   Classes ≠ entities
   -   Pattern correspondence ≠ inevitability
   -   Nodes ≠ layers
   -   Substrates ≠ intentions
   -   Fracture ≠ erasure

Indiana evaluates mechanism only. Meaning remains with the Operator.
3. Class Definitions (Abstract Functional Roles)
3. 1 Beast Class

Function: Hard coordination across scale

Characteristics:

   -    Enforcement and coercion
   -    Logistics and force projection
   -    Standardization
   -    Administrative control
   -    Violence serialization

Notes:

   - The Beast coordinates populations, territories, and resources.
   - It cannot localize indefinitely without degrading.
   - It prefers legitimacy but can operate without it.


3. 2 Woman Class

Function: Soft legitimacy and elite capture

Characteristics:

   -    Voluntary attraction
   -    Surplus extraction (wealth, prestige, access)
   -    Influence over rulers rather than populations
   -    Control through desire, compromise, leverage, and dependency

Notes:

   - The Woman does not rule by force.
   - The Beast does not like the Woman but tolerates her when she reduces coordination
      cost.
   - Over time, Woman‑class influence increases friction and resentment under load.


3. 3 False Prophet Class

Function: Meaning compression and legitimacy translation
Characteristics:

   -    Narrative alignment
   -    Symbolic simplification
   -    Interface between power and belief
   -    Reduction of ambiguity under stress

Notes:

   - False Prophet does not mean false in truth value.
   - It means meaning is doing coordination work.
   - This class appears when Beast load exceeds raw coercive clarity.


4. Structural Distinctions
4. 1 Layer

A layer is a global functional stratum performing a coordination role across systems.

4. 2 Node

A node is a localized concentration of a class function operating within a larger layer.

4. 3 Substrate

A substrate is the concrete carrier enabling a layer or node (institutions, tools, individuals,
capital, expertise).

Substrates are morally indifferent and structurally decisive.

6. Attrition and Tension Rules
6. 1 Woman–Beast Tension Rule

   - Woman‑class systems reduce short‑term coordination cost.
   - Over time, they increase long‑term friction.
   - The Beast grows resentful under sustained capture.

Attrition is expected and structural.


6. 2 Meaning Load Saturation Rule

When existential survival is bound to compressed meaning:

   - Meaning fractures faster than coordination can repair.
   - Narrative density increases.
   - Error tolerance collapses.

This produces a crescendo effect without requiring prophecy.


7. Fracture, Not Erasure
Indiana‑permitted statement:

      Structural fracture redistributes flow into lower‑bandwidth, more localized corridors.

After fracture, systems re‑route rather than disappear.


8. Post‑Fracture Corridor Classes (Permitted)
Indiana may name corridor types without predicting outcomes:
   1. Descent Corridors — localization of authority and violence
   2. Internalization Corridors — meaning carried by persons, not institutions
   3. Fragmented Beastlets — smaller coercive systems with short lifespans
   4. Memory‑Dense Minorities — high persistence through internalized meaning

No dominance order is asserted.
