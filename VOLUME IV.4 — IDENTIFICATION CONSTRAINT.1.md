# **SET IV — CONVERGENCE ARCHITECTURE**

# **VOLUME IV.4 — IDENTIFICATION CONSTRAINT**

*(Canonical Form — Pre-Identification Filter)*

---

## **I. Purpose**

To establish:

**What conditions must be satisfied for any subject to qualify as a valid candidate capable of resolving the aligned and bridged constraint set**

---

## **II. Boundary**

This document assumes:

* Set I is complete  
* Set II is complete  
* Set III is complete  
* IV.0 interface conditions are defined  
* IV.1 independence is established  
* IV.2 constraint alignment is established  
* IV.3 bridging necessity is established

---

This document does NOT:

* identify any subject  
* assert that any subject exists  
* introduce narrative, historical, or theological claims

---

It establishes only:

**the constraints any valid subject must satisfy**

---

## **III. Definition — Identification Constraint**

The identification constraint is:

**the requirement that any candidate subject must satisfy all previously established constraints simultaneously without contradiction**

---

A valid subject must:

* satisfy Set I requirements  
* satisfy Set II requirements  
* satisfy the necessity of bridging (IV.3)

---

Failure at any point:

**eliminates the candidate**

---

## **IV. Required Properties of a Valid Subject**

---

### **IV.1 Identity Authority**

From Set II:

* identity is determined by the ground

From IV.3:

* identity must be applied across rupture

---

**Requirement:**

The subject must possess identity-determining authority, not merely receive it.

---

---

### **IV.2 Existence Authority**

From Set II:

* existence is determined by the same ground as identity

From Set I:

* the same embodied person must be restored

---

**Requirement:**

The subject must possess existence-determining authority sufficient to re-instantiate embodied persons.

---

---

### **IV.3 Domain Operation**

From Set I:

* the solution must operate within the human domain

From IV.3:

* bridging between ground and system is required

---

**Requirement:**

The subject must be capable of operating within the human domain while not being system-derived.

---

---

### **IV.4 Continuity Across Rupture**

From Set I:

* death introduces rupture

From Set II:

* identity continuity is ground-based

---

**Requirement:**

The subject must maintain identity continuity across death without loss of same-one identity.

---

---

### **IV.5 Non-Substitution**

From Set I:

* the same person must be restored

From Set II:

* identity is non-duplicable

---

**Requirement:**

The subject must not introduce substitution, duplication, or replacement identity in restoration.

---

---

### **IV.6 Extension Without Multiplication**

From Set I:

* the solution must extend beyond one individual

From Set II:

* the ground is singular

---

**Requirement:**

The subject must extend restoration beyond itself without multiplying identity or ground.

---

---

## **V. Elimination Criteria**

Any candidate that:

* lacks identity authority  
* lacks existence authority  
* is purely system-derived  
* cannot operate within the human domain  
* introduces duplication or substitution  
* cannot extend beyond a single individual

---

**Is invalid**

---

## **VI. Result**

The identification constraint produces:

* a fully defined requirement set for a valid subject  
* elimination of all insufficient candidate types  
* a narrowed solution space

---

No specific subject is identified.

---

## **VII. Boundary of IV.4**

This document:

* does not identify any subject  
* does not assert existence of a qualifying subject  
* does not describe how the requirements are fulfilled

---

It establishes only:

**what any valid subject must be capable of**

---
