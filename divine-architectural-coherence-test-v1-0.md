Divine Architectural Coherence Test
A precision framework for evaluating claimed revelation
against divine coherence
Scope

This test evaluates architectural coherence, not social popularity, emotional impact, or
institutional authority. It asks whether a claimed revelation exhibits the structural signature
consistent with a truthful, coherent, morally consistent God and with the canonical
rupture/remnant/stabilization architecture we have empirically mapped.

Output Types

Each criterion yields one of:

   -   Pass: coherent with the architecture.
   -   Fail: contradiction or structural violation.
   -   Indeterminate: insufficient data; cannot infer.
   -   Category Error: claim is not a revelation claim in the relevant sense (e.g., pure poetry,
        purely human commentary).

Decision rule: A single hard Fail in a non-negotiable criterion is disqualifying for divine origin,
unless the contradiction is shown to be only apparent and resolvable without rewriting prior
commitments.


## Part I — Non-Negotiables (Hard Constraints)
These are “cannot be otherwise” if the source is the same God.
1) Divine Veracity Constraint

Claim: God cannot lie or present moral falsehood as truth.
Test: Does the revelation affirm statements that are untrue by its own internal standards or that
require God to be morally duplicitous in essence?

   - Pass: God’s speech is consistently truthful.
   - Fail: God is portrayed as essentially deceptive, duplicitous, or false in moral essence.
   - Indeterminate: translation/semantic ambiguity unresolved.

Note: This does not forbid irony, concealment, parable, or judgment by hardening; it forbids
essential moral duplicity.


2) Divine Moral Coherence Constraint

Claim: God’s moral character is coherent and not self-contradictory.
Test: Does the revelation attribute mutually exclusive moral properties to God such that “good”
and “evil” are both essential to Him?

   - Pass: moral attributes remain coherent.
   - Fail: God’s nature requires moral contradiction.
   - Indeterminate: depends on interpretive tradition.


3) Telos Continuity Constraint

Claim: God’s revelation must cohere with the ultimate end/goal (telos) He establishes.
Test: Does the revelation maintain a coherent end-state for creation that does not reverse or
negate the prior telos of redemption, restoration, and final justice?

   - Pass: telos is continuous (even if unfolded progressively).
   - Fail: telos is reversed, negated, or rendered incoherent relative to prior revelation.
   - Indeterminate: telos not specified.


4) Non-Contradiction of Fulfillment Constraint

Claim: Progressive revelation can clarify and fulfill earlier revelation, but cannot deny a
completed fulfillment as false.
Test: If earlier revelation claims a fulfillment event (or a covenant climax), does the new claim
deny it as not having occurred, or reframe it in a way that nullifies its meaning?
   - Pass: fulfillment is preserved or deepened.
   - Fail: fulfillment is denied or made void.
   - Indeterminate: fulfillment status disputed.

This is where “different dispensations” can exist without contradiction: the form changes, but the
fulfillment logic remains continuous.


## Part II — Canonical Architecture Signature (Structural
Constraints)
These evaluate whether the revelation carries the same rupture governance grammar we
observed across genres (narrative, wisdom, lament, apocalyptic).

5) Rupture Governance Signature (R-8)

A divine revelation—if it addresses collapse/judgment/refinement—tends to exhibit:

   -   R1 Load accumulation
   -   R2 Threshold crossing
   -   R3 Destabilization
   -   R4 Boundary conditions
   -   R5 Separation / marking
   -   R6 Stabilization / re-ordering
   -   R7 Meaning frame disclosed
   -   R8 Latency interval (delay without loss of governance)

Test: When the revelation describes collapse, does it show bounded governance rather than
unstructured chaos?

   - Pass: rupture is bounded, sequenced, and meaning-framed.
   - Fail: rupture is arbitrary, boundaryless, purposeless, or incoherent.
   - Indeterminate: no rupture content.


6) Scale-Invariance Constraint

Claim: If the architecture is divine, it should not be genre- or scale-dependent.
Test: Does the revelation preserve the same governance grammar across scales (individual,
communal, national, cosmic, angelic) where it speaks of them?

   - Pass: same rupture grammar across scales.
   - Fail: governance grammar breaks at certain scales (e.g., cosmic realm becomes chaotic
      or morally inverted).
   - Indeterminate: only one scale addressed.


7) Latency Integrity Constraint

Claim: Delay is not disorder; delay is governed time.
Test: Does the revelation handle delayed justice/stabilization without collapsing into nihilism or
moral contradiction?

   - Pass: latency is morally framed and ultimately accounted for.
   - Fail: latency is treated as evidence that governance is absent or morally incoherent.
   - Indeterminate: latency not addressed.


## Part III — Covenant & Mediation Coherence (Mid-Level
Constraints)
These matter especially when comparing Abrahamic claims.

8) Covenant Continuity Constraint

Test: Does the revelation maintain continuity of covenant identity and promise logic (even if
expanded to nations), or does it sever and replace in a way that breaks the prior covenant arc?

   - Pass: covenant continuity preserved.
   - Fail: covenant arc is broken without coherent fulfillment logic.
   - Indeterminate: covenant concept absent.


9) Mediation Consistency Constraint

Claim: In canonical structure, salvation/refinement typically occurs through mediated means
(ark, lamb, priesthood, cross).
Test: Does the revelation preserve a coherent mediation principle, or does it flatten mediation
into arbitrary decree without justice/mercy convergence?

   - Pass: mediation is coherent.
   - Fail: mediation is arbitrary or internally inconsistent with divine justice/mercy.
   - Indeterminate: salvation not addressed.
10) Justice–Mercy Convergence Constraint

Claim: Canonical architecture repeatedly joins justice and mercy without collapsing either.
Test: Does the revelation preserve convergence, or does it resolve the tension by negating
justice (cheap mercy) or negating mercy (pure force)?

   - Pass: both upheld in coherent union.
   - Fail: one negated or moral contradiction introduced.
   - Indeterminate: not addressed.


## Part IV — Internal Integrity Tests (Textual & Logical)
These do not decide divinity alone, but they constrain claims.

11) Internal Consistency Constraint

Test: Does the revelation contain contradictions that are irreconcilable under its own interpretive
rules?

   - Pass: consistent or resolvable.
   - Fail: irreconcilable contradictions in core claims.
   - Indeterminate: depends on exegesis/variant readings.


12) Semantic Stability Constraint

Test: Does the system require constant redefinition of key terms (God, justice, salvation, sin) to
avoid contradictions?

   - Pass: terms are stable.
   - Fail: meaning drift is required for coherence.
   - Indeterminate: unclear.


13) Authority-Verification Constraint

Test: Does the revelation provide coherent markers for recognizing its authority that do not
self-seal by circularity alone?
    - Pass: recognizable markers exist (prophetic fulfillment, coherence with prior, moral
       integrity, community verification).
    - Fail: “believe because it says so” as sole ground.
    - Indeterminate: markers claimed but unverifiable.

(Important: this is not “prove it scientifically.” It’s “does it give non-circular handles.”)


## Part V — Decision Protocol
Step 1: Classify the claim

    - Is it claiming divine speech, angelic dictation, prophetic revelation, or human wisdom?
       If not a revelation claim, stop (Category Error).

Step 2: Apply Non-Negotiables

If any hard constraint fails: Disqualify (unless contradiction is resolved without rewriting the
prior structure).

Step 3: Apply Architecture Signature

If R-8 fails where it speaks of judgment/refinement: strong negative evidence.

Step 4: Apply Covenant/Mediation

Particularly relevant for Abrahamic comparisons.

Step 5: Rate Outcome

    -   Architecturally Coherent: passes non-negotiables + matches signature strongly.
    -   Architecturally Divergent: fails signature or covenant/mediation coherence.
    -   Architecturally Incompatible: fails non-negotiables (veracity, moral coherence, telos).
    -   Indeterminate: insufficient data.


## Part VI — Built-in Falsifiers
These are “model breakers” for divine-origin claims:

    1. God-essential deception (not strategic judgment, but moral duplicity as essence).
    2. Telos reversal (ultimate end contradicts prior ultimate end).
   3. Fulfillment denial (completed covenant hinge denied as false).
   4. Boundaryless rupture (judgment without constraint, without remnant logic, without
       stabilization trajectory).
   5. Meaning collapse (nihilism as final frame).

Any one of these = architectural incompatibility unless resolved by coherent exegesis.


## Part VII — How this avoids offense and avoids drift
   -   It doesn’t call anyone stupid, evil, or insincere.
   -   It doesn’t rely on slogans.
   -   It tests structure, not tribe.
   -   It allows “Indeterminate” where data is missing.
   -   It distinguishes form variation (allowed) from nature contradiction (disallowed).

#### II. FORMAL SPECIFICATION DOCUMENT
Divine Architectural Coherence Test (DACT) v1. 0
1. Scope

The DACT evaluates whether a claimed revelation is structurally consistent with a truthful,
morally coherent, teleologically unified divine source. It does not assess sociological success or
emotional resonance.


2. Ontological Assumptions (Minimal Set)
   1. God is ontologically simple (not self-contradictory).
   2. God is morally coherent.
   3. God cannot lie in essence.
   4. Divine revelation reflects divine nature.
   5. Progressive revelation may increase clarity but cannot negate prior fulfilled truth.

If any of these are rejected, DACT is not applicable.


3. Definitions
Revelation Claim
A text or message claiming divine origin or binding authority from God.

Hard Constraint
A criterion whose failure implies incompatibility with divine coherence.

Architectural Signature
Recurring structural pattern observed across canonical genres: rupture bounded by
governance.

Telos
The ultimate end-state toward which history is directed.

Fulfillment Event
A previously declared covenant climax presented as completed.


4. Hard Constraints
4. 1 Divine Veracity Constraint
A revelation fails if it requires God to be morally deceptive in essence.

Example Fail: God commands falsehood as intrinsic virtue.

Example Pass: God outmaneuvers evildoers without moral contradiction.


4. 2 Moral Coherence Constraint

God’s attributes must not be logically incompatible.

Fail: God is both essentially unjust and perfectly just in identical sense.

Pass: God judges and shows mercy in differentiated but coherent ways.


4. 3 Telos Continuity Constraint

Ultimate destiny of creation must remain coherent across revelation.

Fail: Earlier revelation promises restored creation; later revelation denies restoration as illusion.

Pass: Later revelation deepens but preserves restoration arc.


4. 4 Fulfillment Integrity Constraint

Completed covenant hinge events cannot be denied.

Fail: A later revelation declares a fulfilled redemptive event never occurred.

Pass: A later revelation clarifies its meaning without negation.


5. Architectural Signature Requirements
Revelation describing rupture must demonstrate:

   1. Accumulated load
   2. Threshold event
   3. Destabilization
   4. Boundary conditions
   5. Separation logic
   6. Stabilization trajectory
   7. Meaning disclosure
   8. Latency interval

Absence of boundary or meaning frame indicates divergence.


6. Scale Invariance Requirement
Governance grammar must hold across:

   -   Individual
   -   Communal
   -   National
   -   Cosmic
   -   Angelic (if addressed)

If cosmic realm becomes morally chaotic → fail.


7. Justice–Mercy Convergence Requirement
Revelation must preserve coherent interaction of justice and mercy.

Fail Conditions:

   - Mercy nullifies justice.
   - Justice eliminates mercy.
   - Moral attributes inverted.


8. Covenant & Mediation Coherence
If prior revelation establishes covenant structure and mediation, subsequent revelation must:

   - Preserve continuity, or
   - Provide coherent fulfillment logic.

Arbitrary divine decree without mediation logic = structural anomaly.
9. Internal Logical Integrity
9. 1 Internal Non-Contradiction

Core claims must not be irreconcilably contradictory.

9. 2 Semantic Stability

Terms must remain stable across the system.

9. 3 Authority Markers

Revelation must provide non-circular indicators of authority (e.g., coherence, fulfillment, moral
integrity).

Pure self-sealing circularity weakens claim.


10. Falsifiers
Any of the following disqualify divine coherence unless resolved:

   1. Essential divine deception.
   2. Telos reversal.
   3. Denial of completed fulfillment.
   4. Boundaryless rupture.
   5. Meaning collapse into nihilism.


11. Classification Outcomes
Architecturally Coherent
Passes hard constraints + signature alignment.

Architecturally Divergent
Fails signature but not hard constraints.

Architecturally Incompatible
Fails hard constraint.
Indeterminate
Insufficient data.


12. Usage Protocol
    1. Identify revelation claim.
    2. Apply hard constraints first.
    3. Apply rupture signature.
    4. Apply covenant/mediation.
    5. Evaluate integrity.
    6. Classify.

No skipping order.
