What was abrogated from the Old
Covenant into the New Covenant?
Our answer is:

What was abrogated was the Old Covenant as a covenantal system.
What was not abrogated was the holiness reality that system revealed.

That is the key distinction.

So when people ask, “Was the law abrogated?” our answer is:

Yes, the Mosaic covenantal system was abrogated in its old form.
But no, holiness itself was not abolished.
Instead, it was fulfilled, completed, and brought to its true form in Christ.

That is the clean answer.


The simple version
The Old Covenant did several things at once.

It gave Israel:

    -   a priesthood
    -   sacrifices
    -   temple access rules
    -   purity laws
    -   covenant penalties
    -   national laws
    -   the Decalogue within the Sinai covenant structure

All of that together formed a single covenantal architecture.

That system was real.
It was given by God.
It was holy.
But it was also temporary, external, and incomplete.
It could:

    -   reveal sin
    -   condemn sin
    -   regulate worship
    -   preserve Israel
    -   point forward to Christ

But it could not finally solve the problem.

It could not:

    -   remove guilt once for all
    -   provide final priesthood
    -   open full access to God
    -   transform the heart from the inside

That is why Christ had to come.


So what exactly ended?
What ended was the Old Covenant system in its covenantal form.

That means the old system no longer stands as the operating arrangement between God and
His people.

So the following things are no longer in force as Old Covenant structures:

    -   repeated animal sacrifices
    -   Levitical priesthood
    -   temple and veil access system
    -   ceremonial purity structure
    -   Mosaic covenant as an integrated legal package
    -   old covenant national administration

That whole structure was fulfilled and brought to an end in Christ.

So in that sense, yes:

the Mosaic covenant was abrogated.
But here is where our answer is different
from a blunt “the law is gone”
We do not say that God’s holiness disappeared.
We do not say that moral reality vanished.
We do not say that Sinai was just thrown away.

Why?

Because the Old Covenant was not meaningless.
It was a beam in the architecture.

That is critical.

In our system, Sinai is Beam 5 — the Covenant Holiness System.
It begins at Exodus and Sinai and runs forward until it is resolved in the Cross and the Spirit.

That means Sinai introduced a real line of tension into redemptive history:

    -   God is holy
    -   sinners are guilty
    -   access is barred
    -   sacrifice is necessary
    -   mediation is required
    -   the veil still stands

That beam is not erased.

It is resolved.

So our answer is not:

    - “Sinai continues unchanged”
       or
    - “Sinai was abolished and no longer matters”

Our answer is:

Sinai was fulfilled.


The phrase that locks our answer in
The corpus gives us the exact sentence we need:

“The holiness grammar is not abolished. It is completed.”

That is probably the single most important sentence for this whole question.

What does it mean?

It means the Old Covenant taught true things about:

      -   holiness
      -   guilt
      -   sacrifice
      -   mediation
      -   access
      -   judgment

Those truths did not become false.

But the form in which those truths were administered has now been surpassed by Christ.

So:

      -   sacrifice is completed in the Cross
      -   priesthood is completed in Christ
      -   the veil is torn
      -   the temple finds fulfillment in Christ
      -   the law is internalized by the Spirit rather than merely externalized on stone

That is why our answer is stronger than just saying “abrogated.”


What about the moral law?
This is where people get tripped up.

When they say “moral law,” they often mean several different things without noticing it.

They might mean:

      -   the eternal holiness of God
      -   the duties built into creation
      -   the Decalogue
      -   the whole Mosaic law
   - the covenant of works principle
   - the rule of life for believers

Those are not all the same thing.

So our answer has to separate them.


What continues?
What continues is the holiness reality God revealed.

God is still holy.
Sin is still sinful.
Love of God and neighbor still matters.
Righteousness still matters.
Murder, adultery, idolatry, theft, falsehood — none of those become acceptable.

So we do not say morality disappeared.


What changed?
What changed is the covenantal administration.

The holiness of God is no longer administered through:

   -   repeated sacrifices
   -   Levitical mediation
   -   temple barriers
   -   old covenant national structure
   -   stone-tablet externality by itself

Instead, that same holiness is now brought to completion in:

   -   Christ’s finished work
   -   Spirit-wrought union
   -   internalized law
   -   new covenant access
   -   final priesthood in Christ

So the right category is not “the moral law remained in exactly the same form.”

The right category is:

the holiness reality remained, but its covenantal form changed because Christ fulfilled it.
Why the beam language matters
This is where our architecture really helps.

If Sinai is a beam, then Sinai is carrying real pressure through history.

That pressure is:

     -   sinners need mediation
     -   guilt must be dealt with
     -   holiness cannot be bypassed
     -   access to God requires resolution

If Christ comes and fulfills Sinai, then one of two things must be true:

Either:

     1. the beam is simply cut off and thrown away

or

     2. the beam terminates in Christ and is resolved there

Our system says the second.

That is why we say:

the beam is not discarded; it is terminated in Christ.

That is a much better answer than just saying “abrogated.”

Because it preserves both things:

     - real discontinuity
     - real fulfillment


So how would we explain this to someone
simply?
Here is the plain version:

The Old Covenant was like a temporary holiness system that taught the truth about sin,
judgment, sacrifice, and access to God, but it could not finally fix the problem. Christ came and
fulfilled that whole system. So the Old Covenant itself is no longer in force as the covenantal
arrangement for God’s people. But the holiness that system revealed did not disappear. It
reached its true goal in Christ and is now carried forward in the New Covenant by the Spirit, not
by the old temple-sacrifice-priesthood structure.

That is the answer.


What we would say to 1689 Federalists
We would agree with them that the Mosaic covenant as a covenantal unit does not continue
unchanged.

But we would tighten the language.

We would say:

Do not stop at “abrogated.”

Say more.

Say:

   -   fulfilled
   -   completed
   -   terminated in Christ
   -   internalized by the Spirit
   -   no longer standing in old covenant efficacy

Because if you only say “abrogated,” it can sound like Sinai was disposable.

And in our system, Sinai was not disposable.

It was a real beam in redemptive history that carried holiness tension until Christ resolved it.


Our final answer
Here is the polished Indiana-style-but-readable answer:

In our theology, the Old Covenant was abrogated as a covenantal system — its
priesthood, sacrifices, temple access, ceremonial administration, and Mosaic legal
structure no longer remain in force as the governing form of God’s relationship with His
people. But what that covenant revealed about holiness, guilt, sacrifice, mediation, and
access to God was not abolished. That holiness grammar was a real beam in redemptive
history, and it is completed in Christ. So the transition from Old Covenant to New
Covenant is not mere cancellation, but fulfillment: the old form passes away because the
reality it pointed to has arrived in Christ and is now internalized by the Spirit.
