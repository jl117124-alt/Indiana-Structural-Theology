# **VOLUME IV.3 — BRIDGING NECESSITY**

*(Canonical Form — Constraint Resolution Requirement)*

---

## **I. Purpose**

To establish:

**That the aligned constraints from Set I and Set II cannot be satisfied without a bridging condition, and that such a bridge is required by necessity rather than assumption**

---

## **II. Boundary**

This document assumes:

* Set I is complete  
* Set II is complete  
* Set III is complete  
* IV.0 interface conditions are defined  
* IV.1 independence is established  
* IV.2 constraint alignment is established

---

This document does NOT:

* identify any subject  
* specify how the bridge is implemented  
* introduce narrative or historical claims

---

It establishes only:

**that a bridging condition is required**

---

## **III. Definition — Bridging Necessity**

Bridging necessity is:

**the condition in which two fully aligned constraint sets cannot be jointly satisfied unless a specific form of connection between domains is present**

---

A bridge is required when:

* constraints originate from distinct domains  
* both must be satisfied simultaneously  
* no direct continuity exists between those domains

---

## **IV. Demonstration of Necessity**

---

### **IV.1 External Ground vs Internal Operation**

From Set II:

* the identity–existence ground is non-derived and not system-dependent

From Set I:

* the solution must operate within the human domain

---

**Conflict:**

* Ground is external to the system  
* Solution must operate within the system

---

**Result:**

These conditions cannot both be satisfied unless:

a valid connection exists between external ground and internal domain

---

---

### **IV.2 Identity Authority vs System Rupture**

From Set II:

* identity is determined by the ground

From Set I:

* death introduces rupture that the system cannot reverse

---

**Conflict:**

* identity authority is external  
* rupture disables system continuity

---

**Result:**

Restoration cannot occur unless:

identity authority is applied across rupture from outside the system

---

---

### **IV.3 Re-Instantiation Requirement**

From Set II:

* existence is determined by the same ground as identity

From Set I:

* the same embodied person must be restored

---

**Conflict:**

* existence originates from the ground  
* restoration must occur within the system

---

**Result:**

Restoration requires:

re-instantiation within the system by the external ground

---

---

### **IV.4 Singularity vs Extension**

From Set II:

* the ground is singular

From Set I:

* the solution must extend beyond one individual

---

**Conflict:**

* one ground  
* many persons requiring restoration

---

**Result:**

Extension cannot occur unless:

a single ground can produce multi-person restoration without duplication

---

---

## **V. Elimination of Non-Bridged Models**

---

### **V.1 External-Only Model**

* Ground remains external  
* No system entry

**Failure:**

Cannot operate within the human domain  
Cannot restore embodied persons

---

### **V.2 System-Only Model**

* Solution arises from within the system

**Failure:**

Cannot determine identity  
Cannot overcome rupture

---

### **V.3 Proxy Model**

* Separate agent acts on behalf of ground

**Failure:**

Cannot guarantee identity continuity  
Introduces substitution risk

---

---

## **VI. Result**

The aligned constraints from Set I and Set II:

* cannot be satisfied independently  
* cannot be satisfied by partial models  
* cannot be satisfied without connection

---

**Therefore:**

A bridging condition between the identity–existence ground and the human domain is required

---

## **VII. Boundary of IV.3**

This document:

* does not define the nature of the bridge  
* does not identify the subject  
* does not describe how bridging occurs

---

It establishes only:

**that bridging is necessary**
