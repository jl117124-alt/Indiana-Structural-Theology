# **SET II — CREATION CORRIDOR**

## **VOLUME II.4 — IDENTITY → EXISTENCE CONVERGENCE**

*(Canonical Form — Refactored from Convergence Layer)*

---

## **I. Purpose**

To determine:

**Whether the ground of identity can be distinct from the ground of existence, or whether convergence is required**

---

## **Boundary**

This document assumes:

* identity requires a singular active ground (Volume II.3)  
* identity must persist as the same one  
* identity must survive rupture

This document does NOT assume:

* self-existence (yet)  
* system-entry  
* any redemptive function

---

---

## **II. Problem Definition**

We distinguish two possible grounds:

---

### **Identity Ground (IG)**

Responsible for:

* determining “this one”  
* preserving same-one identity  
* adjudicating continuity

---

### **Existence Ground (EG)**

Responsible for:

* instantiating existence  
* sustaining existence  
* restoring existence

---

---

## **Question**

**Can IG and EG be distinct, or must they be the same ground?**

---

---

## **III. Option 1 — Distinct Grounds**

---

### **Structure:**

* IG determines identity  
* EG determines existence

---

---

## **IV. Evaluation — Distinct Grounds**

---

### **Test 1 — Instantiation Control**

Identity requires:

actual instantiation of the same one

---

If IG and EG are distinct:

* IG identifies  
* EG instantiates

---

### **Failure:**

EG may:

* instantiate a different instance  
* instantiate multiple instances  
* fail to instantiate

---

### **Result:**

Identity assignment is not binding

---

---

### **Test 2 — Non-Duplication**

Identity requires:

* only one instance of the same one

---

If IG ≠ EG:

* IG cannot enforce uniqueness at instantiation

---

### **Failure:**

* duplication possible  
* substitution possible

---

---

### **Test 3 — Rupture and Restoration**

Under rupture:

* identity must be restored

---

If IG ≠ EG:

* IG identifies  
* EG must re-instantiate

---

### **Failure:**

No guarantee EG restores the correct identity

---

---

### **Test 4 — Coordination Requirement**

Distinct grounds require:

* perfect coordination

---

### **Cases:**

---

#### **Case A — Coordination exists**

→ IG and EG function as one system

---

#### **Case B — No guaranteed coordination**

→ identity fails

---

---

### **Result**

Distinction collapses or fails

---

---

## **V. Elimination Result**

---

**Identity-ground and existence-ground cannot be distinct**

---

---

## **VI. Forced Convergence**

---

**Identity-ground \= existence-ground**

---

---

## **VII. Structural Consequences**

---

The unified ground must:

---

### **Identity Functions**

* individuate  
* preserve same-one  
* adjudicate continuity  
* exclude duplication

---

### **Existence Functions**

* instantiate existence  
* sustain existence  
* restore existence

---

---

## **VIII. Dependency Test**

---

### **Question:**

Can the unified ground depend on a deeper source?

---

---

### **Case — Dependent Ground**

If the ground depends on something else:

* that source determines existence  
* that source must coordinate identity

---

### **Result:**

the deeper source is the true ground

---

---

## **IX. Elimination**

---

**Dependent ground is not ultimate**

---

---

## **X. Final Result**

---

**Identity-ground and existence-ground converge in a single, non-derivative ground**
