# VOLUME IV

THE SACRAMENTAL ECONOMY
### Chapter 1


Why Sacraments Exist
(The Structural Necessity of Visible Covenant Signs)


#### I. Why We Must Begin Here
Before defining baptism or the Lord’s Supper, we must answer a prior question:

Why did Christ institute visible ordinances at all?

If sacraments are treated as optional religious symbols, they become decorative.
If they are treated as mechanical grace-conduits, they become superstitious.
If they are reduced to internal experiences, they dissolve into subjectivism.

Therefore:

We must establish their structural necessity within the Christ-centered covenant architecture.

Redemption in this system is:

   -   Federal.
   -   Historical.
   -   Embodied.
   -   Public.
   -   Covenantal.

If redemption is embodied and historical, then its administration cannot be purely invisible.

Sacraments exist because covenant reality must have covenant markers.
They are not ornamental.
They are structural.


#### II. Redemption Is Embodied, Not Abstract
The Son did not redeem by decree alone.

He assumed flesh.
He obeyed in history.
He died physically.
He rose bodily.

Union with Christ is not an idea.
It is federal relocation enacted in time.

Justification is forensic but historically applied.
Regeneration is ontological but occurs within union in history.

Therefore:

If salvation unfolds in embodied history, its covenant administration must also possess visible
form.

Invisible grace does not eliminate visible order.

Embodiment demands signification.

Sacraments are not concessions to weakness.
They are coherent with incarnation.


#### III. Covenant Always Includes Sign
Throughout redemptive history, divine covenants have included visible signs:

   -   The tree in Eden.
   -   The rainbow after the Flood.
   -   Circumcision under Abraham.
   -   Passover under Moses.

A covenant without sign becomes indistinguishable from private spirituality.
But God does not administer redemption privately.
He administers it covenantally.

A covenant sign:

    -   Marks boundary.
    -   Identifies participants.
    -   Publicly declares allegiance.
    -   Establishes visible differentiation.

Signs do not create covenant reality.
They visibly mark it.

This pattern culminates, not disappears, under Christ.


#### IV. Sign ≠ Mechanism
At this point a collapse must be prevented.

A sacramental sign does not mechanically produce the grace it signifies.

Water does not regenerate.
Bread does not transubstantiate into flesh.
Wine does not infuse justification.

Grace is not stored in matter.

The Spirit unites.
The Spirit regenerates.
The Spirit applies justification.

Sacraments are covenant ordinances instituted by Christ.
They do not operate ex opere operato.

If sign becomes mechanism:

    -   Union collapses into ritual.
    -   Faith becomes unnecessary.
    -   Federal relocation becomes automatic.
    -   The decree/application distinction fractures.

This cannot stand.
#### V. Sign ≠ Empty Symbol
A second collapse must also be rejected.

If sacraments are reduced to bare reminders:

    - Covenant administration becomes psychological.
    - Church boundaries become optional.
    - Christ’s institution becomes sentimental.

Sacraments are not mental triggers.

They are:

    -   Authoritative ordinances.
    -   Covenant boundary markers.
    -   Means of visible identification.
    -   Spirit-used confirmations for believers.

They do not create union.
They confirm and declare it.

Symbol is not substance.
But symbol is not nothing.


#### VI. Word and Sign
Sacraments are never detached from the Word.

The Word proclaims Christ.
The Spirit effectually calls through the Word.
Union occurs through the Spirit’s sovereign act.

Sacraments do not replace proclamation.

They accompany proclamation.

The preached Word creates faith.
The sacramental sign confirms faith.

Word without sign risks disembodied abstraction.
Sign without Word collapses into ritualism.

Christ instituted both.
#### VII. Visibility and the Church
Redemption creates a people.

That people must be identifiable.

If union with Christ is invisible, how is the church publicly recognizable?

Not by decree.
Not by secret election.
Not by inner regeneration alone.

But by:

   - Profession of faith.
   - Baptism.
   - Participation in the Lord’s Supper.

Sacraments mark covenant boundary.

They do not identify the elect infallibly.
They identify the confessing community visibly.

Thus:

Sacraments are necessary for ecclesial order.

Without them:

   - The church dissolves into invisible mysticism.
   - Discipline becomes incoherent.
   - Covenant boundaries blur.


#### VIII. The Interim Nature of Sacraments
Sacraments belong to the present age.

They signify what is real but not yet fully consummated.

They operate in the period between:
Accomplished redemption
and
Final confirmation.

When faith becomes sight, sign ceases.

Sacraments are not eternal ordinances.

They are covenant markers for the age of administration.


#### IX. Guardrails Against Collapse
We explicitly reject:

    1. Sacramental regeneration.
    2. Ex opere operato efficacy.
    3. Infusion-based justification.
    4. Bare memorial reductionism.
    5. Mystical participation without federal structure.
    6. Private spirituality detached from visible covenant.

Sacraments must remain:

    -   Christ-instituted.
    -   Word-governed.
    -   Spirit-dependent.
    -   Faith-received.
    -   Covenant-marking.


#### Chapter Thesis (Locked)
Sacraments exist because redemption, though accomplished invisibly by the Spirit through
union with Christ, is administered covenantally and historically within a visible community;
therefore Christ instituted authoritative, embodied signs that publicly mark covenant boundaries,
confirm faith, and signify spiritual realities without mechanically producing them, preserving both
the distinction between sign and substance and the integrity of Word-governed, Spirit-applied
grace within the interim age of redemptive administration.

THE SACRAMENTAL ECONOMY
### Chapter 2


The Definition of a Sacrament
(Sign, Seal, and Covenant Ordinance)


#### I. Why Definition Is Necessary
Before treating baptism or the Lord’s Supper individually, we must define what a sacrament is.

If definition is vague:

    -   Ritual replaces theology.
    -   Emotion replaces covenant structure.
    -   Mechanism invades grace.
    -   Symbol collapses into sentiment.

If definition is inflated:

    - Matter becomes magical.
    - Grace becomes materialized.
    - Union becomes ritualized.

If definition is reduced:

    - Christ’s institution becomes optional.
    - Covenant boundary dissolves.
    - Church order fragments.

Therefore:

A sacrament must be defined precisely within the Christ-centered covenant architecture already
established.
#### II. Formal Definition
A sacrament is a Christ-instituted, visible covenant ordinance consisting of a tangible sign joined
to the Word, which signifies and seals the benefits of union with Christ to believing participants,
publicly marks covenant identity, and functions within the Spirit-governed administration of the
church without mechanically producing the grace it signifies.

Each component must be preserved.


#### III. Christ-Instituted Ordinance
A sacrament is not:

   - A humanly devised ritual.
   - A symbolic ceremony derived from cultural preference.
   - A later ecclesial development.

It must be:

   - Explicitly instituted by Christ.
   - Given to the church.
   - Normative in redemptive administration.

Divine institution is non-negotiable.

Without Christ’s command, there is no sacrament.

This preserves authority and prevents sacramental inflation.


#### IV. Visible and Tangible Sign
A sacrament contains material elements:

   - Water.
   - Bread.
   - Wine.

These elements are not incidental.
They preserve embodiment.

Redemption is not abstract spirituality.
Therefore its covenant signs are not abstract.

Material signification reflects incarnational coherence.

Yet matter does not contain grace.
The element signifies; it does not transmute.

Sign ≠ substance.
Matter ≠ Spirit.


#### V. Joined to the Word
The sign never stands alone.

The Word:

   -   Defines the meaning.
   -   Announces the promise.
   -   Governs the administration.
   -   Interprets the act.

Without the Word:

The sign becomes ritualism.

Without the sign:

The Word risks disembodied abstraction in covenant administration.

Christ joined command, promise, and act.

Thus sacrament is Word-governed sign.


#### VI. Signifies the Benefits of Union
Sacraments do not create union.

Union with Christ occurs by the Spirit through effectual calling, as previously established in the
doctrine of regeneration.
Therefore:

Sacraments signify:

    -   Cleansing.
    -   Death and resurrection with Christ.
    -   Participation in His body.
    -   Covenant inclusion.
    -   Ongoing communion.

They do not effect federal relocation.

They declare and seal what the Spirit accomplishes.


#### VII. Seal Without Mechanism
The term “seal” must be clarified.

A seal confirms; it does not create.

A seal authenticates; it does not generate.

Sacraments seal:

    - The promise of the Gospel.
    - The covenant identity of the believer.
    - The reality of union.

They do not:

    -   Infuse righteousness.
    -   Produce regeneration.
    -   Automatically justify.
    -   Operate ex opere operato.

If seal becomes mechanism,
justification collapses into ritual.

If seal becomes empty metaphor,
covenant confirmation disappears.

Seal must retain covenantal force without mechanical causation.
#### VIII. Faith as Receiving Instrument
Sacramental participation without faith does not confer grace.

The Spirit applies grace.

Faith receives grace.

The sacrament is:

   - A confirming ordinance for believers.
   - A visible confession of allegiance.
   - A Spirit-used means of strengthening assurance.

Without faith:

The sign does not benefit.

This preserves the already established order:

Union precedes sacrament.
Faith precedes proper participation.
Grace is Spirit-applied, not element-generated.


#### IX. Public Covenant Function
Sacraments are ecclesial acts.

They are:

   -   Public.
   -   Corporate.
   -   Administered within the church.
   -   Governed by recognized authority.

They are not private devotional exercises.

They function as:

   -   Boundary markers.
   -   Identity declarations.
   -   Covenant affirmations.
   -   Jurisdictional identifiers.
This preserves visible church integrity.


#### X. Negative Clarifications
We explicitly reject:

    1. Sacramental regeneration.
    2. Transubstantiation or ontological element change.
    3. Ex opere operato efficacy.
    4. Symbolic minimalism that reduces sacrament to memory aid.
    5. Individualized administration detached from the church.
    6. Covenant membership without credible faith.

Each rejection protects a prior doctrinal lock.


#### XI. Structural Integration
This definition preserves:

    -   Federal relocation at union.
    -   Regeneration within union.
    -   Justification applied in time.
    -   Sign ≠ Mechanism.
    -   Word priority.
    -   Spirit sovereignty.

No previously established distinction is violated.

Sacraments operate within the already established redemptive order.
They do not reconfigure it.


#### Chapter Thesis (Locked)
A sacrament is a Christ-instituted, Word-governed, visible covenant ordinance consisting of a
tangible sign that signifies and seals the benefits of union with Christ to believing participants,
publicly marks covenant identity within the church, and functions as a Spirit-used means of
confirmation without mechanically producing regeneration, justification, or federal relocation,
thereby preserving the distinction between sign and substance and maintaining the integrity of
grace as sovereignly applied through union.


Interpreter Mode.
Steady.
No drift.
Gold standard maintained.


THE SACRAMENTAL ECONOMY
### Chapter 3


Sacraments and Union with Christ
(Federal Relocation and Covenant Signification)


#### I. Why This Chapter Is Necessary
If sacraments are not properly located in relation to union with Christ, one of two collapses
occurs:

   1. Sacraments create union.
   2. Sacraments are detached from union entirely.

The first produces sacramental regeneration.
The second produces symbolic minimalism.

Neither is consistent with the architecture already established.
Union with Christ has been defined as:

   -   Federal relocation from Adam to Christ.
   -   Spirit-wrought.
   -   Occurring in history through effectual calling.
   -   The locus of regeneration and justification application.

Therefore:

Sacraments must be positioned relative to union without altering union’s priority.


#### II. Union Is Spirit-Effected, Not Ritual-Generated
Union with Christ occurs when the Spirit effectually calls, regenerates, and incorporates the
sinner into Christ.

This incorporation is:

   -   Monergistic.
   -   Sovereign.
   -   Immediate.
   -   Ontological and federal.

No material element effects this transfer.

Water does not relocate federal headship.
Bread does not alter covenant standing.

Federal relocation precedes sacramental administration.

If sacrament caused union:

   -   Faith would become secondary.
   -   The Spirit’s sovereignty would be displaced.
   -   Justification would become ritualized.
   -   The decree/application distinction would fracture.

Therefore:

Union is logically and causally prior to sacrament.
#### III. Sacraments Presuppose Union
Baptism and the Lord’s Supper both assume participation in Christ.

Baptism signifies:

    -   Death with Christ.
    -   Burial with Christ.
    -   Resurrection with Christ.
    -   Identification with Christ.

The Lord’s Supper signifies:

    - Communion with Christ.
    - Participation in His body and blood.
    - Ongoing covenant fellowship.

These realities presuppose prior union.

The sacrament does not say:

“Become united.”

It declares:

“You are united.”

Thus sacrament functions as visible covenant confirmation of invisible federal reality.


#### IV. No Pre-Union Sacramental Efficacy
Because union precedes sacrament, the sacrament has no saving efficacy apart from faith.

Participation without union:

    - Does not justify.
    - Does not regenerate.
    - Does not relocate federal standing.

The unbelieving participant receives the sign, but not the reality signified.

This preserves:

    - Spirit sovereignty.
   - Faith priority.
   - The regenerate church framework.

Sacrament does not operate in advance of union.

It confirms what the Spirit has wrought.


#### V. Faith and Sacramental Participation
Faith is the first conscious expression of regenerated life.

Therefore:

Proper sacramental participation requires credible profession.

Faith is not produced by sacrament.
Faith receives what sacrament signifies.

This prevents:

   - Infant inclusion without union.
   - Covenant membership detached from regeneration.
   - Two-tier covenant collapse.

Sacrament is administered to those who confess Christ because it signifies participation in Him.


#### VI. Federal Logic Maintained
Federal headship governs this structure.

Before union:

   - In Adam.
   - Under condemnation.
   - Outside covenant blessing.

At union:

   - Relocated to Christ.
   - Justified.
   - Regenerated.
    - Incorporated into the covenant community.

Sacrament marks that relocation publicly.

It does not perform the relocation.

If sacrament performed relocation:

Federal transfer would become ritualized.

But federal transfer is Spirit-wrought, not element-mediated.

The logic remains intact.


#### VII. Union and the Visible Church
Union with Christ creates the invisible reality.

Sacraments create visible identification.

The church cannot see union infallibly.

It recognizes:

    - Profession of faith.
    - Baptism.
    - Participation in the Supper.

Thus:

Sacraments function as covenant boundary markers in the visible order, while union remains the
decisive invisible reality.

Visible administration does not replace spiritual incorporation.

It declares it.


#### VIII. Warning Passages and False Participation
Scripture warns against unworthy participation.

This proves:
Sacraments do not automatically confer grace.

One may receive the sign while lacking union.

Judgment passages reinforce the distinction:

    - Participation ≠ union.
    - Administration ≠ regeneration.
    - Visibility ≠ election.

This preserves ecclesial seriousness without sacramental mysticism.


#### IX. Guardrails Against Collapse
We explicitly reject:

    1. Sacramental union.
    2. Ritual-based federal relocation.
    3. Faith-independent covenant inclusion.
    4. Invisible-only ecclesiology.
    5. Mechanical grace transfer.

Union remains Spirit-wrought.
Sacrament remains covenant sign.

The order does not invert.


#### Chapter Thesis (Locked)
Union with Christ is the sovereign, Spirit-effected federal relocation from Adam to Christ that
occurs through effectual calling in history, and sacraments neither create nor precede this union
but presuppose and visibly signify it, functioning as covenant confirmations for those who have
been incorporated into Christ by faith, thereby preserving the priority of union, the sovereignty of
the Spirit, and the distinction between invisible spiritual reality and visible ecclesial
administration.

THE SACRAMENTAL ECONOMY
### Chapter 4


The Nature of the Church
(Visible Assembly, Covenant Boundary, and Regenerate Membership)


#### I. Why This Chapter Must Come Here
Sacraments are administered in the church.

Therefore, if the church is undefined, sacramental theology collapses immediately into one of
three errors:

   1. Mysticism — the “real church” is invisible only, so sacraments become optional
       symbolism.
   2. Mechanism — sacraments create the church, so ritual replaces union.
   3. Nominalism — the church is cultural affiliation, so sacraments become social customs.

This volume is operating under a regenerate-church sacramental framework.

Therefore:

We must define the church’s nature before we define who receives baptism and who receives
the Supper.

Without this chapter:

   - Covenant boundary becomes arbitrary.
   - Discipline becomes incoherent.
   - Sacramental administration becomes self-authorized.

So we begin here.
#### II. Definition of the Church
The church is the covenant assembly of those united to Christ, publicly gathered under His
authority, constituted by the Word, and marked by confession, baptism, and table participation
within a visible order governed by Christ-appointed oversight.

This definition has two dimensions:

   - Invisible reality: union with Christ.
   - Visible administration: gathered assembly and covenant marks.

We preserve both without collapsing them.


#### III. Invisible Reality: Union with Christ
The true church exists because union with Christ exists.

Union is:

   - Spirit-wrought federal relocation from Adam to Christ.
   - Historically applied through effectual calling.
   - The locus of regeneration and justification application.

Therefore:

The church is not first a human organization.
It is first a Christ-created people.

This preserves:

Christ as head, not church as engine.


#### IV. Visible Administration: Public Covenant Assembly
Although union is invisible, the church is not merely invisible.

The church is visible because:

   - The Gospel is preached publicly.
   - Confession is made publicly.
   - Baptism is administered publicly.
    - The Supper is received publicly.
    - Discipline is enacted publicly.

Visibility is not optional.

A purely invisible church has no boundary and no governance.

Therefore:

The church is an assembled, identifiable body in history.


#### V. The Church Is Covenantal, Not Voluntary Association
The church is not merely a club of shared preferences.

It is a covenant assembly created by Christ’s authority.

Membership is not grounded in:

    -   Personal taste.
    -   Cultural inheritance.
    -   Ethnic continuity.
    -   Familial tradition.
    -   Civil belonging.

Membership is grounded in:

    - Union with Christ confessed by faith.
    - Public identification through baptism.
    - Ongoing communion through the Supper.

This preserves the church as covenantal reality rather than social construct.


#### VI. Regenerate Membership and Credible Profession
In this framework, the church is constituted by confessing believers.

Because:
Sacraments signify union with Christ.
Union produces faith.
Therefore sacramental administration presupposes confession.

Thus:

Church membership is not generational by default.

We do not claim infallible access to election.

But we do claim:

The church’s visible boundary is governed by credible profession.

This preserves:

   - The integrity of baptism.
   - The seriousness of the table.
   - The coherence of discipline.


#### VII. The Marks of the Church
The church is recognized and maintained by Christ-appointed means:

   1. The Word rightly preached
       The Gospel defines the church.
       Without the Word, the church becomes an institution without Christ’s voice.
   2. The ordinances rightly administered
       Baptism and the Supper mark covenant identity and sustain covenant life.
   3. Discipline rightly exercised
       Boundary enforcement is not optional.
       Without discipline, covenant markers become meaningless.

These marks are not decorative.
They are load-bearing.


#### VIII. Church Authority and Administration
The church operates under Christ’s authority.

This authority is not personal charisma, not mere majority vote, not mystical succession.
It is exercised through:

    -   Teaching.
    -   Shepherding.
    -   Oversight.
    -   Discipline.

The sacraments require authorized administration because they are covenant acts, not private
ceremonies.

Without jurisdiction:

    - Anyone can baptize into anything.
    - The table becomes private consumption.
    - Covenant boundary dissolves.

Therefore:

Visible church authority is required for sacramental integrity.


#### IX. The Church and the World
The church is in the world but not identical to the world.

Therefore:

    - Civil belonging ≠ church membership.
    - Cultural Christianity ≠ union with Christ.
    - Attendance ≠ covenant participation.

The church is a differentiated people:

    -   Marked by confession.
    -   Marked by baptism.
    -   Sustained at the table.
    -   Governed by discipline.

This is not elitism.

It is covenant boundary.
#### X. Guardrails Against Collapse
We explicitly reject:

    1. Invisible-only ecclesiology
        (which dissolves sacraments and discipline)
    2. Institution-as-church reduction
        (which detaches church from union)
    3. Sacraments-create-membership mechanism
        (which ritualizes federal relocation)
    4. Generational presumption
        (which detaches membership from faith)
    5. Autonomous administration
        (which destroys jurisdiction)

The church must remain:

    -   Christ-headed.
    -   Word-constituted.
    -   Sacrament-marked.
    -   Discipline-governed.
    -   Publicly visible.


#### Chapter Thesis (Locked)
The church is the Christ-created covenant assembly of those united to Him by Spirit-wrought
federal relocation, made visible in history through public confession of faith, gathered under the
Word, and maintained by the right administration of baptism and the Lord’s Supper and by
covenant discipline, such that the church’s visible boundary is governed by credible profession
rather than generational presumption, preserving both the invisible reality of union and the
visible order required for sacramental integrity and ecclesial jurisdiction.


THE SACRAMENTAL ECONOMY
### Chapter 5


Baptism
(The Sign of Federal Relocation and Covenant Identification)


#### I. Why We Must Begin Here
Baptism stands at the threshold of visible church membership.

If baptism is misunderstood:

   -   Union collapses into ritual.
   -   Church membership detaches from faith.
   -   Covenant boundary blurs.
   -   Sacramental regeneration re-enters.
   -   Or baptism is reduced to mere symbolism.

Because this volume has established:

   -   Union is Spirit-wrought federal relocation.
   -   Regeneration occurs within union.
   -   Justification is applied at union.
   -   Sacraments signify and seal but do not create grace.
   -   The church is constituted by confessing believers.

Therefore:

Baptism must be defined in a way that preserves union’s priority, faith’s necessity, and covenant
order.


#### II. Definition of Baptism
Baptism is the Christ-instituted covenant ordinance in which a believer is immersed in water in
the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, publicly signifying and sealing
union with Christ in His death and resurrection, cleansing from sin, transfer of covenant
headship, and incorporation into the visible church.
Each clause must stand.


#### III. Christ-Instituted and Trinitarian
Baptism is not apostolic invention.

It is instituted by the risen Christ.

It is administered in the Triune Name.

Therefore:

Baptism is:

    - Christ-authorized.
    - Trinitarian.
    - Covenantally binding.

Its authority derives from Christ’s command, not ecclesial creativity.


#### IV. The Meaning of the Sign
Baptism signifies multiple, unified realities grounded in union with Christ:

1. Death and Resurrection with Christ

Immersion signifies:

    - Burial with Christ.
    - Rising to new life.
    - Identification with His redemptive work.

This presupposes prior union.

The water does not kill the old man.
The Spirit does.

The water does not raise to life.
The Spirit does.

The sign declares what union accomplishes.
2. Cleansing from Sin

Water signifies washing.

But cleansing is not material.

The blood of Christ cleanses.
The Spirit applies.

Water signifies purification;
it does not effect it.


3. Federal Relocation

Baptism marks transfer of allegiance:

    - From Adam to Christ.
    - From world to church.
    - From prior identity to covenant identity.

It does not perform the relocation.

It publicly declares the relocation already effected by the Spirit.

This preserves federal coherence.


4. Incorporation into the Visible Church

Baptism is not merely individual testimony.

It is ecclesial incorporation.

Through baptism:

    - The believer publicly identifies with Christ.
    - The church publicly recognizes the believer.
    - Covenant membership becomes visible.

Thus baptism is boundary marker.
#### V. Baptism Presupposes Faith
Because baptism signifies union with Christ, and union produces faith, baptism presupposes
credible profession.

Faith is:

    - The first conscious expression of regeneration.
    - The instrument of justification.
    - The public confession of allegiance.

Therefore:

Baptism follows faith.

It does not create faith.
It does not precede faith.
It does not replace faith.

This preserves:

    - Regenerate church logic.
    - Union priority.
    - Spirit sovereignty.


#### VI. Baptism Does Not Regenerate
We explicitly reject:

    1. Baptismal regeneration.
    2. Automatic covenant inclusion by ritual.
    3. Element-mediated grace transfer.
    4. Justification through water.

Regeneration is Spirit-wrought within union.

Justification is forensic and applied at union.

Baptism is sign and seal.

It confirms; it does not cause.
#### VII. The Mode of Baptism
Immersion most clearly reflects the symbolism of burial and resurrection.

While the efficacy of baptism does not depend on water quantity, the sign should correspond as
closely as possible to the reality signified.

Immersion best preserves:

     - Death symbolism.
     - Resurrection imagery.
     - Cleansing imagery.

The sign should not contradict its meaning.


#### VIII. The Timing of Baptism
Baptism belongs at the beginning of the believer’s visible discipleship.

It is:

     -   Initiatory.
     -   Not repeatable.
     -   Not progressive.
     -   Not optional.

It marks entry into the visible covenant community.

It is not:

     - A maturity badge.
     - A later upgrade.
     - A ritual for spiritual advancement.

One union, one baptism.


#### IX. Baptism and Assurance
Baptism does not create assurance.

But it serves as:
    - Public marker of allegiance.
    - Tangible reminder of union.
    - Visible testimony of faith.

It strengthens faith because it directs the believer back to Christ’s finished work.

The assurance rests in Christ, not in the water.


#### X. Guardrails Against Collapse
We explicitly reject:

    1. Sacramental regeneration.
    2. Infant baptism detached from profession.
    3. Ritual-based covenant membership.
    4. Private self-baptism.
    5. Baptism detached from church oversight.

We affirm:

    -   Christ-instituted authority.
    -   Faith prerequisite.
    -   Union priority.
    -   Covenant boundary clarity.
    -   Visible ecclesial incorporation.


#### Chapter Thesis (Locked)
Baptism is the Christ-instituted, Trinitarian covenant ordinance administered upon credible
profession of faith, which visibly signifies and seals Spirit-wrought union with Christ in His death
and resurrection, cleansing from sin, and federal relocation from Adam to Christ, publicly
incorporating the believer into the visible church without regenerating, justifying, or effecting
union, thereby preserving the priority of Spirit-applied grace and the integrity of covenant
boundary within the regenerate assembly.


THE SACRAMENTAL ECONOMY
### Chapter 6


The Lord’s Supper
(Covenant Communion, Participation, and Judicial Seriousness)


#### I. Why We Must Begin Here
If the Lord’s Supper is misunderstood, one of three distortions emerges:

   1. Transubstantiation or ontological transformation
       (the elements become Christ’s body and blood in substance)
   2. Sacramental mechanism
       (grace is automatically infused through reception)
   3. Bare memorialism
       (the Supper is reduced to a mental reminder)

All three distortions violate previously established doctrine.

Because this volume has already affirmed:

   -   Union is Spirit-wrought federal relocation.
   -   Sacraments signify and seal but do not create grace.
   -   The church is a visible covenant assembly.
   -   Word governs sacrament.

Therefore:

The Lord’s Supper must be defined in a way that preserves real participation without ontological
change, covenant seriousness without mechanism, and remembrance without reduction.


#### II. Definition of the Lord’s Supper
The Lord’s Supper is the Christ-instituted covenant ordinance in which baptized believers
partake of bread and wine as visible signs of Christ’s body and blood, proclaiming His death,
participating in covenant communion with Him and one another, renewing corporate allegiance,
and receiving Spirit-mediated nourishment without re-sacrificing Christ or effecting union.

Every clause must stand.


#### III. Christ-Instituted Covenant Meal
The Supper originates in Christ’s command.

It is:

     -   Instituted on the night of His betrayal.
     -   Connected to the Passover.
     -   Given to the gathered disciples.
     -   Commanded to be continued “until He comes.”

Therefore:

The Supper is:

     - Covenantally binding.
     - Ecclesially administered.
     - Eschatologically oriented.

It is not optional devotional symbolism.

It belongs to Christ’s church until consummation.


#### IV. The Meaning of the Elements
The bread signifies Christ’s body given for His people.
The cup signifies His blood shed for the forgiveness of sins.

The elements:

     -   Represent His once-for-all sacrifice.
     -   Do not repeat it.
     -   Do not transform into it.
     -   Do not contain Christ materially.
The Cross is complete.
Justice is satisfied.
No additional sacrifice occurs.

The Supper proclaims; it does not reenact atonement.


#### V. Proclamation and Remembrance
The Supper proclaims the Lord’s death until He comes.

Remembrance in Scripture is not mere mental recollection.

It is covenantal remembering.

To remember covenantally is:

    -   To rehearse promise.
    -   To reaffirm allegiance.
    -   To renew commitment.
    -   To stand again under declared grace.

Thus:

The Supper is remembrance,
but not reduction to memory.

It is proclamation embodied.


#### VI. Real Participation Without Ontological Change
Scripture speaks of participation (koinonia) in the body and blood of Christ.

Participation must not be denied.

But participation must be defined properly.

Participation is:

    - Spiritual communion with the risen Christ.
    - Mediated by the Spirit.
    - Received by faith.
    - Grounded in prior union.

It is not:

    - Physical ingestion of divine substance.
    - Ontological alteration of bread and wine.
    - Automatic infusion of grace.

Christ is bodily present in heaven.

The Supper is real communion by the Spirit, not local embodiment in the elements.

Thus:

Participation is covenantal and spiritual, not material and metaphysical.


#### VII. Covenant Renewal and Corporate Unity
The Supper is corporate.

“One bread, one body.”

It declares:

    -   Shared union.
    -   Shared redemption.
    -   Shared allegiance.
    -   Shared identity.

Divisiveness at the table contradicts its meaning.

Thus:

The Supper is not private spirituality.

It is corporate covenant participation.


#### VIII. Judicial Seriousness and Covenant Sanction
This section must be explicit.

Scripture warns that those who partake unworthily:
    -   Eat and drink judgment.
    -   May become weak.
    -   May become ill.
    -   May even die.

This establishes that the Supper is not casual symbolism.

But judgment does not prove ontological transformation of elements.

It proves covenant seriousness.

The Supper is a covenant meal.

Covenant meals carry sanctions.

To partake unworthily is to:

    -   Profane Christ’s sacrifice.
    -   Mock covenant unity.
    -   Treat holy ordinance as common consumption.
    -   Sin against the body and blood of the Lord.

Judgment flows from covenant violation, not from mystical properties in bread.

The danger lies in profaning what Christ instituted, not in mishandling metaphysical matter.

Thus:

The Supper is neither magic nor trivial.

It is holy covenant participation.


#### IX. Worthy Participation
Worthy participation does not require sinlessness.

It requires:

    -   Self-examination.
    -   Discernment of the body.
    -   Repentant faith.
    -   Recognition of covenant seriousness.

The Supper is for baptized believers walking in repentance.
It is not:

    - For the unconverted.
    - For the indifferent.
    - For the self-exalting.

Discipline and table fencing protect this integrity.


#### X. Nourishment and Assurance
The Supper strengthens believers.

Not because grace resides in elements.

But because:

    -   The Spirit uses visible proclamation.
    -   Faith is reinforced through embodied sign.
    -   Covenant promises are rehearsed.
    -   Corporate communion encourages perseverance.

The nourishment is spiritual.

It is grounded in Christ.

It is received by faith.


#### XI. Guardrails Against Collapse
We explicitly reject:

    1. Transubstantiation or consubstantiation.
    2. Re-sacrificial theology.
    3. Sacramental regeneration through the Supper.
    4. Ex opere operato grace infusion.
    5. Bare memorial minimalism.
    6. Casual or private administration detached from church authority.

We affirm:

    - Real participation.
   -   Spirit-mediated communion.
   -   Covenant seriousness.
   -   Corporate unity.
   -   Proclamation until Christ returns.


#### Chapter Thesis (Locked)
The Lord’s Supper is the Christ-instituted covenant meal in which baptized believers partake of
bread and wine as visible signs of His once-for-all sacrifice, proclaiming His death, participating
spiritually in communion with Him and one another through the Spirit, renewing covenant
allegiance, and receiving faith-strengthening nourishment, while bearing real covenantal
accountability such that unworthy participation invites divine discipline—not because the
elements change in substance, but because the ordinance is a holy act of covenant communion
within the visible church.


THE SACRAMENTAL ECONOMY
### Chapter 7


Sacramental Efficacy
(How Grace and Sign Relate Without Mechanism or Reduction)


#### I. Why This Chapter Is Necessary
After defining baptism and the Lord’s Supper, we must now answer a decisive question:
How do sacraments actually function?

If we do not define efficacy clearly, the system collapses in one of two directions:

   1. Mechanism — sacraments automatically confer grace.
   2. Minimalism — sacraments do nothing beyond symbolic recollection.

Both distortions contradict prior doctrine.

Because we have already established:

   -   Union is Spirit-wrought federal relocation.
   -   Regeneration occurs within union.
   -   Justification is forensic and applied at union.
   -   Sacraments signify and seal but do not create union.

Therefore:

Sacramental efficacy must be defined in a way that preserves real spiritual benefit without ritual
causation.


#### II. What Sacramental Efficacy Is Not
Before defining what sacramental efficacy is, we must exclude what it cannot be.

Sacraments do not:

   -   Regenerate.
   -   Justify.
   -   Effect federal relocation.
   -   Infuse grace ex opere operato.
   -   Operate apart from faith.
   -   Function independently of the Word.

If sacraments caused grace automatically:

   -   Faith would become unnecessary.
   -   Union would become ritualized.
   -   The Spirit’s sovereignty would be displaced.
   -   Decree and application would collapse into ceremony.

This is impossible within our system.
#### III. The Positive Definition of Sacramental Efficacy
Sacramental efficacy is the Spirit-mediated strengthening, confirming, and nourishing of
believers through Christ-instituted visible signs, whereby the promises of the Gospel are
embodied, proclaimed, and applied to faith without altering the order of union or the ground of
justification.

Each component must be preserved.


#### IV. Word, Spirit, and Sign
Sacraments never operate alone.

They function within a triadic structure:

   1. The Word — defines promise and proclaims Christ.
   2. The Spirit — applies grace and produces faith.
   3. The Sign — visibly embodies and confirms the promise.

The Word creates faith.
The Spirit unites to Christ.
The sign strengthens and confirms.

Remove the Word — ritual remains.
Remove the Spirit — symbolism remains.
Remove the sign — embodied confirmation is absent.

Efficacy lies not in matter, but in Spirit-governed covenant administration.


#### V. The Role of Faith
Faith is the receiving instrument.

Sacraments benefit only those who believe.

This does not mean faith creates the grace.

Grace is already secured in Christ and applied in union.

But faith receives what the sacrament confirms.
Without faith:

   - The sign is received.
   - The grace signified is not embraced.
   - Judgment may result (as previously established).

Thus:

Efficacy is conditional upon faith, not because faith empowers the sacrament, but because faith
is the means by which grace is received.


#### VI. Sacraments as Means of Grace
Sacraments are properly called means of grace.

But this phrase must be defined.

They are means of grace in that:

   -   The Spirit uses them to strengthen believers.
   -   They confirm promises visibly.
   -   They deepen assurance.
   -   They reinforce union-consciousness.
   -   They sustain covenant identity.

They are not means of grace in that:

   - They produce regeneration.
   - They justify apart from faith.
   - They alter federal standing.

The means are confirmatory, not causative.


#### VII. Efficacy and Assurance
Sacraments support assurance.

Not because they add to Christ’s work.

But because:
   - They direct believers back to Christ’s finished work.
   - They visibly display covenant promises.
   - They engage embodied memory and corporate solidarity.

The efficacy is pastoral and covenantal.

It is not metaphysical transformation.


#### VIII. Efficacy and Discipline
Sacraments also function as means of discipline.

When received improperly:

   - They expose hypocrisy.
   - They reveal unbelief.
   - They invite divine correction.

Thus sacramental efficacy includes both:

   - Strengthening grace for believers.
   - Judicial seriousness for the profane.

This dual aspect reinforces covenant integrity.


#### IX. The Eschatological Dimension of Efficacy
Sacraments operate within the interim age.

They sustain believers between:

   - Accomplished redemption
      and
      Final consummation.

Their efficacy is temporary and provisional.

When faith becomes sight:

   - Confirmation is no longer required.
   - Embodied sign yields to unveiled presence.
Sacraments strengthen pilgrims, not the confirmed glorified.


#### X. Guardrails Against Collapse
We explicitly reject:

    1. Ex opere operato causation.
    2. Element-based grace storage.
    3. Symbolic trivialization.
    4. Sacramental autonomy apart from the Word.
    5. Faithless covenant inclusion.
    6. Mystical union through ingestion.

We affirm:

    -   Spirit sovereignty.
    -   Faith as receiving instrument.
    -   Word-governed signification.
    -   Confirmatory efficacy.
    -   Covenant seriousness.


#### Chapter Thesis (Locked)
Sacramental efficacy consists in the Spirit-mediated strengthening, confirmation, and
nourishment of believers through Christ-instituted, Word-governed visible signs, such that the
promises of the Gospel are embodied and applied to faith without regenerating, justifying, or
effecting union, thereby preserving the sovereignty of the Spirit, the priority of union with Christ,
and the distinction between sign and substance within covenant administration.


THE SACRAMENTAL ECONOMY
### Chapter 8


Discipline and Sacramental Governance
(Covenant Boundary, Jurisdiction, and Holy Administration)


#### I. Why This Chapter Is Necessary
Sacraments are covenant acts.

Covenant acts require:

   - Authority.
   - Boundary.
   - Accountability.

If sacramental governance is undefined:

   -   Anyone may administer baptism without oversight.
   -   The Lord’s Table becomes private consumption.
   -   Covenant identity becomes self-declared.
   -   Discipline collapses into suggestion.

Because this volume has already established:

   -   The church is a visible covenant assembly.
   -   Baptism marks entry.
   -   The Supper renews covenant participation.
   -   Sacraments carry judicial seriousness.

Therefore:

Sacraments require governance.

Without governance, there is no covenant boundary.


#### II. Christ’s Authority Over the Church
The church does not own the sacraments.
Christ does.

He instituted them.
He defines their meaning.
He commands their administration.

All sacramental governance flows from Christ’s headship.

The church exercises derivative authority.

Not autonomous authority.
Not creative authority.
Not cultural authority.

Derivative. Delegated. Accountable.


#### III. Jurisdiction and Administration
Sacraments are not private religious exercises.

They are ecclesial acts.

Therefore:

They must be administered under recognized church authority.

This includes:

   - Recognized leadership.
   - Orderly procedure.
   - Public accountability.

Self-baptism contradicts covenant order.

Private table observance detached from church fellowship fractures covenant visibility.

Sacraments belong to the gathered church, not isolated individuals.


#### IV. Baptism and Membership Governance
Baptism marks entry into the visible church.
Therefore:

The church must:

   - Examine credible profession.
   - Guard against false confession.
   - Administer baptism under oversight.

Baptism is not:

   - A spontaneous personal rite.
   - A family tradition.
   - A cultural ceremony.

It is covenant incorporation.

Thus governance ensures:

   - Faith precedes administration.
   - Order precedes recognition.
   - Oversight precedes incorporation.

Without examination, the boundary collapses.


#### V. The Lord’s Table and Fencing
The Lord’s Supper is restricted to baptized believers walking in repentance.

Because:

The Supper is covenant renewal.
Covenant renewal presupposes covenant membership.

Therefore:

The church must fence the table.

Table fencing includes:

   -   Warning against unworthy participation.
   -   Calling for self-examination.
   -   Restricting the unrepentant.
   -   Excluding the disciplined.
This is not hostility.

It is covenant faithfulness.

Failure to guard the table invites judgment.


#### VI. Discipline as Covenant Protection
Church discipline is not optional policy.

It is covenant enforcement.

Discipline exists to:

    -   Preserve doctrinal purity.
    -   Guard moral integrity.
    -   Protect sacramental holiness.
    -   Call sinners to repentance.

If discipline is removed:

    - Sacraments lose boundary.
    - The church loses identity.
    - Covenant seriousness evaporates.

Discipline is restorative, not vindictive.

But it is real.


#### VII. Exclusion and Restoration
Persistent unrepentance requires exclusion from the table.

Exclusion is:

    - Public recognition of covenant breach.
    - Withdrawal of sacramental participation.
    - Call to repentance.

Restoration is equally covenantal.

Upon repentance:
    - Confession is acknowledged.
    - Participation is restored.
    - Communion resumes.

Sacraments therefore operate within a framework of covenant accountability.


#### VIII. Governance and Unity
Sacramental governance protects unity.

Without boundary:

    - False teaching spreads.
    - Hypocrisy multiplies.
    - Division deepens.

Governance preserves:

    - Doctrinal integrity.
    - Moral seriousness.
    - Visible coherence.

Authority does not create unity.

It protects it.


#### IX. Civil Authority and Church Authority
The church’s sacramental authority is spiritual, not civil.

Civil governments do not:

    - Define sacramental meaning.
    - Grant or revoke baptism.
    - Control table access.

Christ alone governs His church.

Church authority is limited to:

    - Word.
    - Sacrament.
    - Discipline.

It does not extend to civil coercion.

This preserves proper jurisdictional distinction.


#### X. Guardrails Against Collapse
We explicitly reject:

    1. Autonomous private sacramental practice.
    2. State-controlled sacramental authority.
    3. Unexamined baptismal inclusion.
    4. Open table without discernment.
    5. Discipline-less ecclesiology.
    6. Authoritarian abuse detached from Scripture.

We affirm:

    -   Christ’s headship.
    -   Ordered administration.
    -   Faith-governed participation.
    -   Accountable leadership.
    -   Covenant boundary maintenance.


#### Chapter Thesis (Locked)
Sacraments, as Christ-instituted covenant ordinances marking and renewing participation in His
visible church, require ordered governance under His delegated authority, such that baptism is
administered upon examined profession, the Lord’s Table is fenced and guarded, and discipline
is exercised to preserve doctrinal purity, moral integrity, and covenant seriousness, thereby
maintaining the visible boundary of the regenerate assembly without usurping Christ’s headship
or collapsing ecclesial authority into civil power.

THE SACRAMENTAL ECONOMY
### Chapter 9


Old Covenant Signs and Fulfillment
(Continuity, Discontinuity, and Christological Completion)


#### I. Why This Chapter Is Necessary
Sacramental theology cannot be complete without addressing redemptive-historical continuity.

If the relation between old covenant signs and new covenant ordinances is undefined, several
collapses occur:

   1. Radical discontinuity — the new covenant floats free from prior covenant structure.
   2. Flat continuity — old covenant administration transfers unchanged into the new.
   3. Sacramental confusion — circumcision and baptism are treated as identical in function
       and scope.

Because this volume has already established:

   -   Union with Christ is the decisive covenant relocation.
   -   Baptism presupposes faith and union.
   -   The church is constituted by credible profession.
   -   Sacraments signify and seal but do not effect federal transfer.

We must now articulate continuity and discontinuity without contradiction.


#### II. Covenant Signs in the Old Covenant
Old covenant signs included:
   -   Circumcision.
   -   Passover.
   -   Temple sacrifices.
   -   Ceremonial washings.

These signs functioned to:

   -   Mark covenant boundary.
   -   Distinguish Israel from the nations.
   -   Point forward to redemptive fulfillment.
   -   Structure corporate worship and identity.

They were:

   - Divinely instituted.
   - Covenantally binding.
   - Temporally administered.

They were not ultimate.

They were preparatory.


#### III. Circumcision and Its Function
Circumcision marked:

   - Inclusion within Abrahamic covenant lineage.
   - Participation in national covenant identity.
   - Obligation to covenant law.

It was administered to male infants on the basis of covenant promise to Abraham’s offspring.

Important distinctions:

Circumcision did not guarantee regeneration.
It did not ensure saving faith.
It marked national covenant membership.

Thus old covenant administration included:

   - Mixed membership.
   - External covenant inclusion without internal renewal.

This historical reality must not be ignored.
#### IV. Passover and Covenant Meal Structure
Passover functioned as:

    -   Memorial of deliverance.
    -   Corporate covenant meal.
    -   National identity marker.
    -   Redemptive rehearsal.

It pointed forward to Christ.

It did not repeat deliverance.
It commemorated and participated covenantally in prior redemption.

This structure informs but does not control the Lord’s Supper.


#### V. Fulfillment in Christ
Christ fulfills:

    -   The promises to Abraham.
    -   The typology of circumcision.
    -   The sacrificial system.
    -   The Passover deliverance.

Fulfillment does not erase covenant structure.

It transforms administration.

The new covenant is:

    - Not national but ecclesial.
    - Not ethnic but faith-defined.
    - Not anticipatory but accomplished.

Christ does not abolish signification.

He re-centers it in Himself.
#### VI. Discontinuity in Covenant Boundary
The decisive discontinuity concerns covenant membership.

Old covenant inclusion was:

    - Genealogical.
    - National.
    - Mixed (believing and unbelieving).

New covenant inclusion is:

    - Confessional.
    - Christ-centered.
    - Defined by union through faith.

This does not mean the church is perfectly regenerate in visibility.

But the boundary principle changes.

Membership is no longer determined by physical descent.

It is determined by credible profession of faith in Christ.

Therefore:

Circumcision and baptism cannot be treated as administratively identical.


#### VII. Continuity in Sign Structure
There is real continuity:

    -   God continues to use visible signs.
    -   Covenant identity remains marked publicly.
    -   Covenant meals remain central.
    -   Word and sign remain joined.

The structure persists.

But the referent shifts from anticipation to fulfillment.

Circumcision pointed forward to heart renewal.

Baptism signifies union with the One who accomplishes heart renewal.
Passover pointed forward to sacrificial deliverance.

The Lord’s Supper proclaims the sacrifice already accomplished.


#### VIII. Why Infant Inclusion Does Not Transfer
Because old covenant inclusion was genealogical and national, infant circumcision functioned
within that framework.

But the new covenant is defined by:

    - Union with Christ.
    - Spirit-wrought regeneration.
    - Credible profession of faith.

Baptism signifies union with Christ.

Therefore:

It presupposes faith.

The structural basis for infant inclusion no longer governs covenant boundary.

This is not a denial of continuity.

It is recognition of administrative transformation under fulfillment.


#### IX. The Temporary Nature of Old Covenant Signs
Old covenant signs were:

    - Typological.
    - Preparatory.
    - Temporally bound.

With Christ’s fulfillment:

    - Sacrificial system ceases.
    - Temple centrality ceases.
    - National boundary ceases.

New covenant signs remain only those instituted by Christ:
    - Baptism.
    - The Lord’s Supper.

No further sacramental expansion is authorized.


#### X. Guardrails Against Collapse
We explicitly reject:

    1. Total discontinuity that severs the church from covenant history.
    2. Flat continuity that transfers genealogical inclusion unchanged.
    3. Sacramental identity between circumcision and baptism.
    4. National-theocratic reconfiguration of the church.
    5. Replacement theology that denies Abrahamic fulfillment in Christ.

We affirm:

    -   Christological fulfillment.
    -   Structural continuity of visible signs.
    -   Administrative discontinuity in membership principle.
    -   Faith-defined covenant boundary.
    -   Word-governed sacramental economy.


#### Chapter Thesis (Locked)
Old covenant signs such as circumcision and Passover functioned as divinely instituted,
genealogically administered markers of national covenant identity that pointed forward to
redemptive fulfillment in Christ; under the new covenant, Christ’s accomplished work transforms
administration so that baptism and the Lord’s Supper remain as visible covenant ordinances,
preserving structural continuity of signification while redefining covenant boundary around union
with Christ and credible profession of faith rather than physical descent, thereby maintaining
fulfillment without collapsing into either radical discontinuity or genealogical continuity.


THE SACRAMENTAL ECONOMY
### Chapter 10


The Eschatological End of Sacraments
(Sign, Substance, and the Termination of Interim Administration)


#### I. Why This Chapter Is Necessary
Sacraments belong to redemptive administration in history.

If their temporal nature is not defined, two distortions emerge:

   1. Perpetual sacramentalism — sacraments become eternal necessities.
   2. Present over-realization — sacraments are treated as if consummation has already
       arrived.

Because this volume has already established:

   -   Sacraments signify union with Christ.
   -   They strengthen faith.
   -   They mark covenant boundary.
   -   They function within the visible church.
   -   They operate under Spirit-mediated administration.

We must now define their endpoint.

Sacraments belong to the age of faith, not the age of sight.


#### II. The Nature of Sacraments as Interim Ordinances
Sacraments operate between:

   - Accomplished redemption (the Cross and Resurrection),
      and
   - Final consummation (glorification and confirmed glory).
They are:

   -   Confirmatory.
   -   Strengthening.
   -   Boundary-marking.
   -   Proclamatory.

They are not:

   - Ultimate realities.
   - Permanent necessities.
   - Eternal mediators.

They exist because the church lives in the “already” and “not yet.”


#### III. Sign and Substance
Sacraments are signs.

Signs exist because the substance is not yet fully unveiled.

Baptism signifies:

   - Union with Christ.
   - Cleansing.
   - Death and resurrection.

The Lord’s Supper signifies:

   - Communion with Christ.
   - Covenant fellowship.
   - Participation in redemption accomplished.

But in consummation:

   - Union is fully realized.
   - Cleansing is complete.
   - Communion is immediate.

When the substance is directly experienced, the sign is no longer required.
#### IV. Faith and Sight
Sacraments function in the economy of faith.

Faith receives what is not yet seen.

The Supper proclaims “until He comes.”

This establishes its temporal boundary.

When Christ returns:

    - Proclamation gives way to presence.
    - Mediation gives way to unveiled communion.
    - Sign gives way to direct participation.

Sacraments are suited to pilgrimage, not confirmed glory.


#### V. The End of Baptism
Baptism marks entry into the visible covenant community.

In the eternal state:

    - There is no evangelistic incorporation.
    - No new conversions.
    - No boundary between visible and invisible church.

All the redeemed are fully gathered and confirmed.

Thus:

Baptism belongs to the age of gospel mission.

It does not extend into eternal consummation.


#### VI. The End of the Lord’s Supper
The Lord’s Supper proclaims Christ’s death “until He comes.”

At His return:
    - Proclamation ceases.
    - The wedding supper of the Lamb replaces interim meal.
    - Covenant renewal gives way to covenant consummation.

The Supper strengthens faith in absence.

In presence, strengthening through sign is no longer required.

Communion becomes direct and unmediated.


#### VII. The Confirmed State
Earlier doctrine established the distinction:

Innocence ≠ confirmed glory.

Sacraments belong to the probationary and pilgrim state.

In confirmed glory:

    -   Sin is no more.
    -   Discipline is no more.
    -   Fencing the table is no more.
    -   Mixed membership is no more.

The church is perfected.

Sacramental governance ceases because covenant risk ceases.


#### VIII. No Eternal Sacramental Mediation
We explicitly reject:

    1. Eternal sacramental priesthood.
    2. Ongoing sacrificial reenactment.
    3. Perpetual element-based mediation.

Christ’s priesthood is eternal.

Sacramental signs are not.

They are instruments of interim administration.
They do not continue beyond the need for faith-confirmation.


#### IX. The Telos of the Sacramental Economy
The sacramental economy is ordered toward Christ Himself.

Its purpose is not ritual preservation.

Its purpose is:

    -   Sustaining believers.
    -   Guarding covenant boundary.
    -   Strengthening faith.
    -   Proclaiming redemption.

When redemption is fully unveiled:

The economy completes its function.

The goal is not sacraments.

The goal is Christ.


#### X. Guardrails Against Collapse
We explicitly reject:

    1. Over-realized eschatology that trivializes present sacraments.
    2. Under-realized eschatology that eternalizes sacramental forms.
    3. Confusion between wedding supper imagery and ongoing sacramental administration.
    4. The notion that sacraments complete redemption.

We affirm:

    -   Sacraments belong to the present age.
    -   They are temporary yet necessary.
    -   They serve faith, not replace it.
    -   They terminate at consummation.
#### Chapter Thesis (Locked)
Sacraments are Christ-instituted covenant ordinances belonging to the interim administration of
redemption between accomplished atonement and final consummation, functioning as visible
signs that strengthen faith, mark covenant boundary, and proclaim Christ’s work until His return;
when faith gives way to sight and union with Christ is fully realized in confirmed glory, the
necessity of sacramental mediation ceases, for the substance to which they pointed is directly
possessed in the unveiled presence of the Lord.

This addendum exists to engage directly and comprehensively the principal New Testament

This addendum operates under the following non-negotiables:

   1. Scripture interprets Scripture.
   2. Clear didactic passages about justification and regeneration govern sacramental
       language.
   3. No single text may overturn the doctrine of justification by faith grounded in Christ’s
       finished work.
   4. Sacramental language must be read in covenantal context, not as isolated ritual
       metaphysics.
   5. Strong language does not automatically imply mechanistic causation.

We are not protecting a philosophical system.
We are protecting the coherence of the Gospel.


## PART I — BAPTISM TEXTS

#### III. John 3:5 — “Born of Water and the Spirit”
1. Contextual Framing

Jesus rebukes Nicodemus as “the teacher of Israel” for failing to understand new birth
language.

Therefore, the conceptual background must be available in the Hebrew Scriptures.

2. Old Testament Background

Ezekiel 36:25–27 promises:

   -   cleansing (water imagery),
   -   new heart,
   -   Spirit indwelling,
   -   covenant renewal.
Water and Spirit are paired in prophetic restoration language.

Thus “water and Spirit” can coherently describe one regenerative act of covenant cleansing and
renewal.

3. Does This Require Baptismal Causation?

No.

Reasons:

      - Jesus emphasizes Spirit sovereignty (“The wind blows where it wishes”).
      - The passage centers on divine initiative, not ritual submission.
      - The new birth is “from above,” not from human administration.

The text supports:

      - Regeneration as cleansing and Spirit-renewal,
      - Baptism later as the covenant sign of that reality,

without requiring water to cause new birth.

4. Pastoral Clarity

Jesus is not giving Nicodemus a ritual prescription.
He is confronting him with his inability and the necessity of divine transformation.

The “how” is Spirit sovereignty, not sacramental procedure.


#### IV. Acts 2:38 — “Repent and Be Baptized… for the
Forgiveness of Your Sins”
1. Narrative Context

Acts consistently portrays:

      - Gospel proclamation,
      - Repentance/faith,
      - Baptism as public incorporation.

2. The Preposition Eis
While often purpose/result oriented, eis in covenantal proclamation can indicate movement into
a publicly recognized state.

The text presents repentance and baptism as a unified conversion-initiation event.

The question is not whether they are linked (they are), but whether baptism is the efficient cause
of forgiveness.

3. Canonical Constraint

Elsewhere in Acts:

   - The Spirit falls before baptism (Acts 10).
   - The Spirit comes after belief but before baptism (Acts 10:44–48).
   - Baptism precedes Spirit reception in some instances (Acts 8).

If baptism were the necessary efficient cause of forgiveness and Spirit reception, Luke’s
narrative would not display such variation.

Therefore Acts 2:38 must be read as covenantally integrated proclamation, not ritual causation.

4. Positive Statement

Baptism is:

   -   Commanded.
   -   Urgent.
   -   Normative.
   -   Public covenant entry.

But forgiveness remains grounded in Christ’s finished work and received by repentant faith.


#### V. 1 Peter 3:21 — “Baptism Now Saves You”
This is the strongest baptismal text.

1. Peter’s Immediate Qualification

“Not as removal of dirt from the body…”

Peter explicitly rejects physical-mechanical causation.

2. The Nature of the Saving
He continues:

“…but as an appeal to God for a good conscience, through the resurrection of Jesus Christ.”

The saving efficacy is:

   - Through resurrection.
   - Through appeal.
   - Through covenant identification.

3. The Ark Typology

Noah:

   - Was righteous before the flood.
   - Passed through judgment via the ark.
   - Was preserved by God’s appointed means.

The ark did not generate righteousness.
It was the divinely appointed instrument of deliverance through judgment.

Baptism parallels:

   - Passage through judgment,
   - Identification with the righteous One,
   - Entry into new covenant life.

This is covenantal instrumentality grounded in Christ—not element-based regeneration.

4. Strong Conclusion

Peter affirms real salvific language.
But he locates power in resurrection and covenant appeal, not in water as material cause.


## PART II — EUCHARISTIC TEXTS

#### VI. John 6 — “Unless You Eat… and Drink…”
1. Literary Context
John 6 occurs before the institution narrative.

Jesus identifies Himself as the bread of life.

He equates:

    -   Coming to Him,
    -   Believing in Him,
    -   Eating His flesh,
    -   Drinking His blood.

These metaphors are intertwined.

2. Internal Clarification

Jesus says:

“The flesh profits nothing; the words I have spoken to you are spirit and life.”

The emphasis is:

    - Total dependence,
    - Faith-union,
    - Appropriation of His life.

3. Eucharistic Relevance

While later Eucharistic theology rightly hears resonance, the discourse itself defines eating and
drinking in terms of believing.

Therefore:

John 6 supports deep, life-giving participation in Christ—
but does not require element transformation.


#### VII. 1 Corinthians 10:16–21 — Participation (Koinonia)
1. The Analogy with Idolatry

Paul warns against idol feasts because participation binds worshiper to what is represented.

Participation is real and relational.
2. Nature of Participation

The text does not describe ontological ingestion of demons.

It describes:

   - Covenant solidarity,
   - Spiritual alignment,
   - Real relational communion.

Thus Christian participation is:

   -   Real,
   -   Spiritual,
   -   Covenantally binding,
   -   Spirit-mediated.

3. Strengthening the Language

The Supper does more than confirm prior union.

It expresses, deepens, and enacts communion within union.

Participation is dynamic, not static.


#### VIII. 1 Corinthians 11:27–34 — Judgment
Paul attributes weakness and death to unworthy participation.

The cause is:

   - Profaning the covenant meal,
   - Failing to discern the body,
   - Divisive conduct.

This establishes:

   - Real holiness,
   - Real covenant sanctions.

It does not establish:

   - Ontological change of elements,
   - Automatic infusion of grace.
Judgment arises from covenant violation, not element chemistry.


## PART III — COVENANT CONTINUITY &
DISCONTINUITY

#### IX. Jeremiah 31 — New Covenant Membership
Jeremiah prophesies:

“They shall all know me… from the least to the greatest.”

The new covenant differs from the old in internalization and knowledge of the Lord.

This does not eliminate visible church mixture.

But it signals:

A shift from genealogical covenant inclusion
to covenant membership defined by internal knowledge.

This grounds the shift in baptismal administration.


#### X. Colossians 2:11–12
Paul links:

    - Circumcision of Christ (spiritual),
    - Burial with Him in baptism,
    - Faith in God’s working.

The emphasis is on spiritual fulfillment.

Paul is not issuing administrative instructions regarding infants.

He is showing that what circumcision anticipated is fulfilled in union with Christ.

Continuity of meaning does not require identity of recipients.
#### XI. Household Baptisms
Household narratives describe:

   - Word spoken to the house,
   - Rejoicing in belief,
   - Baptism following response.

The texts do not specify ages.

They cannot serve as decisive proof for infant inclusion.

Nor do they prove infant exclusion.

They are narratively consistent with household conversion and public identification.


## PART IV — SYNTHESIS

#### XII. Positive Sacramental Realism
The New Testament teaches:

   - Baptism is tightly linked to forgiveness and salvation language.
   - The Supper is real participation.
   - Unworthy reception brings judgment.

Therefore:

Sacraments are not empty symbols.

But none of the texts require:

   - Element-based regeneration,
   - Automatic grace transfer,
   - Ontological element transformation.

The strongest texts always locate saving power in:
   -   Christ’s resurrection,
   -   Faith,
   -   Spirit agency,
   -   Covenant appeal.


#### XIII. The Coherent Model
A coherent biblical synthesis affirms:

   1. Regeneration is Spirit-wrought.
   2. Justification is grounded in Christ’s finished work.
   3. Baptism is covenantal incorporation and saving identification.
   4. The Supper is real communion and covenant renewal.
   5. Participation is spiritual and covenantal, not materialistic.

This preserves:

   - Gospel centrality,
   - Sacramental seriousness,
   - Canonical coherence.


# Addendum Thesis (Final Form)
The strongest sacramental texts of the New Testament—while employing vivid language of
cleansing, saving, eating, drinking, participation, and judgment—do not necessitate a
mechanistic or element-based causation model; rather, when read within their covenantal,
Christ-centered, and canonical contexts, they affirm a robust sacramental realism in which
baptism and the Lord’s Supper are Spirit-governed, Word-bound ordinances that truly
incorporate, nourish, and discipline within the visible church, while grounding all saving efficacy
in the resurrected Christ and received by faith.


ECONOMY
# ADDENDUM 0


The Christological Hermeneutic Governing
Sacramental Exegesis
(Center, Canon, and Doctrinal Hierarchy)


#### I. Why This Addendum Is Necessary
Recent critique has suggested that the sacramental doctrine presented in this volume is
governed primarily by a Pauline forensic framework—specifically, justification by faith—and that
sacramental texts are subordinated to that grid.

This requires clarification.

The governing center of this system is not a single doctrinal formulation.

It is the Person and Work of Jesus Christ.

Christ is:

    -   The eternal Son.
    -   The revealer of the Father.
    -   The head of the Church.
    -   The mediator of the covenant.
    -   The one in whom all things hold together.
    -   The telos of creation and redemption.

All doctrine—justification, regeneration, ecclesiology, sacramental theology—exists under Him.

This addendum clarifies the hermeneutical hierarchy operative throughout Volume IV.


#### II. The Governing Center: Christ as Telos
The New Testament is not concept-centered but Christ-centered.

Doctrinal loci derive coherence from:
    -   The identity of Christ,
    -   The finished work of Christ,
    -   The exalted lordship of Christ,
    -   The Spirit’s application of Christ.

Therefore:

Justification does not govern sacramental theology as an abstract principle.

Christ governs sacramental theology as the living mediator.

Sacraments are Christ-instituted ordinances.

They derive meaning, efficacy, and limit from Him.


#### III. Canonical Hierarchy Without Canonical Reduction
The New Testament contains multiple loci:

    -   Kingdom proclamation (Gospels).
    -   Narrative expansion (Acts).
    -   Forensic and union language (Paul).
    -   Priestly and sacrificial fulfillment (Hebrews).
    -   Ethical and communal emphasis (James).
    -   Abiding and life language (John).

Polycentric emphasis does not eliminate hierarchy.

Hierarchy is Christological:

No text may contradict the finished work of Christ.
No ordinance may compete with the sufficiency of Christ.
No sign may displace the substance who is Christ.

This is not privileging one author over another.

It is privileging the Son over all subordinate categories.


#### IV. The Role of Justification Within This Hierarchy
Justification by faith:
   - Safeguards the sufficiency of Christ’s atonement.
   - Protects grace from human contribution.
   - Clarifies the legal application of Christ’s righteousness.

It does not function as the metaphysical center of the canon.

It functions as a necessary soteriological guardrail.

Sacramental language must cohere with justification not because justification dominates
Scripture, but because Christ’s finished work cannot be contradicted.

Thus:

Sacraments may be instrumental in covenant administration,
but they may not compete with Christ as ground of righteousness.


#### V. Sacramental Texts as Genuine Didactic
Sacramental texts are not illustrative rhetoric.

They carry real doctrinal weight.

When Scripture says:

   -   “Baptism now saves you,”
   -   “Repent and be baptized for forgiveness,”
   -   “The cup is participation in the blood of Christ,”
   -   “Unless you eat… you have no life,”

these are not disposable metaphors.

They are covenantal teaching.

This system does not reduce them to mere reminders.

Rather, it reads them as:

   -   Christ-governed,
   -   Spirit-mediated,
   -   Covenantally serious,
   -   Instrumental within administration,
   -   Non-mechanistic in causation.
#### VI. Instrumentality Properly Defined
The sacraments are:

   - Ordinary means of grace,
   - Instruments of covenant incorporation and communion,
   - Means by which Christ administers His church.

They are not:

   - Efficient causes of regeneration,
   - Independent generators of union,
   - Competitors with the finished work of Christ.

Union with Christ is Spirit-wrought.

Sacraments instrumentally participate in:

   - The visible administration of that union,
   - The strengthening of faith within that union,
   - The public covenantal incorporation of believers.

Instrumentality here refers to covenant administration under Christ’s authority, not ontological
initiation independent of the Spirit.


#### VII. Order of Being and Order of Knowing
A crucial distinction undergirds this volume:

   - The order of being (Spirit-wrought union precedes sacramental participation).
   - The order of knowing and experiencing (sacramental participation strengthens, confirms,
      and deepens communion).

Scripture often speaks phenomenologically—describing what occurs in covenant participation
without pausing to delineate metaphysical sequence.

Systematic theology clarifies without diminishing.

Thus:

“Baptism saves” affirms covenantal instrumentality.
It does not negate that the saving ground is Christ’s resurrection.
#### VIII. Acts 2:39 and Generational Promise
Peter declares:

“The promise is for you and for your children and for all who are far off, everyone whom the Lord
our God calls to Himself.”

This affirms:

   - Generational scope of promise,
   - Missional expansion,
   - Divine calling as determinative.

The promise is not genealogically automatic.

Its realization rests in divine calling and response of repentance and faith.

Therefore, baptism remains tied to repentant response under the new covenant administration.


#### IX. Summary Clarification
This sacramental theology is governed by:

Christ as ontological center and covenant mediator.

It safeguards:

   -   The sufficiency of His atonement.
   -   The sovereignty of the Spirit in union.
   -   The seriousness of covenant ordinances.
   -   The integrity of the visible church.

It does not subordinate sacramental texts to Pauline abstraction.

It synthesizes sacramental language under the lordship of the Son.


# Addendum 0 Thesis (Locked)
The doctrine of the sacraments in this volume is governed not by a single forensic formulation
but by a Christological hermeneutic in which the Person and Work of Jesus Christ function as
the ontological and canonical center of Scripture, such that sacramental texts are received with
full doctrinal weight as Spirit-mediated, covenantal instruments within Christ’s administration of
His church, while all saving efficacy remains grounded in His finished work and sovereignly
applied by the Spirit.


ECONOMY
# ADDENDUM II


Pastoral Clarifications on Assurance,
Conversion, Credible Profession, and
Sacramental Practice
(Experience, Order, and Ecclesial Discipline)


#### I. Why This Addendum Is Necessary
Sacramental theology does not remain theoretical.

It shapes:

   -   How believers understand their conversion.
   -   How they approach baptism.
   -   How they approach the Lord’s Table.
   -   How elders examine profession.
   -   How assurance is cultivated or distorted.

Because this volume has:

   - Distinguished union from sacrament,
   - Affirmed Spirit sovereignty,
   - Rejected mechanistic causation,
    - Affirmed sacramental instrumentality within covenant administration,

pastoral clarification is necessary to prevent:

    1. Decisionism.
    2. Sacramental trivialization.
    3. Inquisitorial gatekeeping.
    4. Assurance collapse.
    5. Ritual superstition.

This addendum addresses those risks.


#### II. Assurance and the Ground of Confidence
1. The Ground of Assurance

Assurance rests fundamentally in:

    - The finished work of Christ.
    - The promises of God in the Gospel.
    - The faithfulness of God to His covenant.

It does not rest in:

    -   The moment of baptism.
    -   The intensity of experience.
    -   The memory of a decision.
    -   The precision of doctrinal articulation.

Sacraments support assurance.

They do not create its ground.

2. Sacraments as Strengtheners of Assurance

Baptism and the Lord’s Supper function pastorally to:

    -   Anchor faith in visible obedience.
    -   Reinforce union realities.
    -   Proclaim Christ’s sufficiency.
    -   Rehearse covenant identity.

They do not authenticate election.
They confirm faith by directing believers back to Christ.


#### III. Gradual Conversions and the Order of Experience
1. The Distinction

The Spirit’s work may be:

   - Sudden and datable.
   - Gradual and progressive.
   - Unclear in temporal boundary.

Union with Christ occurs decisively in reality.

But awareness of that union may unfold gradually.

The absence of a dramatic conversion narrative does not invalidate genuine faith.

2. Raised-in-the-Church Believers

Those raised within the church may not know:

A time when they did not trust Christ.

This does not contradict:

   - Spirit-wrought regeneration.
   - The necessity of personal faith.
   - The requirement of credible profession.

The decisive question is present trust in Christ, not recollection of a crisis moment.


#### IV. Credible Profession — Definition and Limits
1. What Credible Profession Is

Credible profession consists of:

   - Articulate trust in Christ.
   - Understanding of the Gospel’s core.
   - Evidence of repentance.
    - Willing submission to Christ’s lordship.
    - Public identification with the church.

It does not require:

    - Exhaustive theological mastery.
    - Emotional intensity.
    - Perfect moral maturity.

2. What Credible Profession Is Not

It is not:

    -   A psychological exam.
    -   An infallible judgment of election.
    -   A guarantee of perseverance.
    -   A declaration of moral perfection.

The church recognizes profession.

It does not decode the heart.


#### V. Avoiding Decisionism
Because this system insists union precedes sacrament, a danger emerges:

Reducing conversion to a remembered decision.

This must be rejected.

Faith is:

    - Spirit-produced.
    - Ongoing.
    - Trusting reliance upon Christ.

The focus is not:

“When did you decide?”

The focus is:

“Do you presently trust Christ?”
Baptism marks public covenant entry.

It does not memorialize a psychological moment.


#### VI. Avoiding Sacramental Trivialization
Opposing mechanistic sacramentalism must not collapse into triviality.

The sacraments are:

   -   Holy ordinances.
   -   Christ-commanded.
   -   Covenantally serious.
   -   Spirit-used means of grace.

Irreverence, casual participation, or reduction to bare symbolism is error.

The Supper carries discipline.

Baptism carries covenant obligation.


#### VII. Fencing the Table Without Inquisition
The Lord’s Supper must be guarded.

But guarding must avoid two extremes:

   1. Open table without discernment.
   2. Excessive scrutiny that binds consciences.

The biblical requirement is:

Self-examination,
Discernment of the body,
Repentant faith.

Elders are responsible to guard the table publicly.

They are not granted omniscience.
#### VIII. Church Discipline and Covenant Integrity
Sacramental participation implies:

   - Accountability.
   - Mutual submission.
   - Correction where necessary.

Church discipline exists to:

   - Restore the erring.
   - Protect the congregation.
   - Preserve Christ’s honor.

Excommunication removes access to the Supper because the visible sign of union cannot
coexist with visible repudiation of Christ.

This is covenant coherence, not punitive ritualism.


#### IX. Children in the Covenant Community
Children of believers:

   -   Are to be taught.
   -   Are to be nurtured.
   -   Are to be instructed in the Gospel.
   -   Are to be called to personal faith.

They are not treated as pagans.

They are not presumed regenerate.

They are covenantally instructed and invited toward credible profession.

Baptism awaits that profession.


#### X. Ordinary Means and Spiritual Growth
The Word and Sacraments are ordinary means of grace.

This means:
    - Christ ordinarily nourishes faith through them.
    - Believers ordinarily grow under their administration.
    - The Spirit ordinarily uses them in sanctification.

Ordinary does not mean mechanically guaranteed.

It means Christ-appointed and Spirit-used.


#### XI. The Affective Weight of Sacramental Language
The New Testament speaks of sacraments with strong language:

    -   Washing.
    -   Saving.
    -   Participation.
    -   Eating and drinking life.

Pastoral practice must preserve that weight.

Therefore:

Baptism is preached as covenant entry into Christ.
The Supper is preached as real communion with Christ.

But saving power is always located in:

    - Christ crucified and risen.
    - The Spirit’s application.
    - Faith’s reception.

The sacraments direct attention upward, not inward and not toward the elements themselves.


#### XII. Final Guardrails
We explicitly reject:

    -   Sacramental magic.
    -   Sacramental minimalism.
    -   Decisionist reduction.
    -   Emotional elitism.
    -   Inquisitorial gatekeeping.
   - Ritual superstition.

We affirm:

   -   Christ-centered assurance.
   -   Spirit-wrought faith.
   -   Public covenant obedience.
   -   Reverent sacramental practice.
   -   Pastoral patience.
   -   Ecclesial accountability.


# Addendum II Thesis (Locked)
Pastorally applied, the doctrine of the sacraments must direct believers’ assurance to the
finished work of Christ, recognize the Spirit’s varied patterns of conversion, require credible
profession without psychological absolutism, preserve sacramental holiness without ritual
mechanism, and administer baptism and the Lord’s Supper as Christ-instituted, Spirit-used
means of covenant incorporation and communion within the visible church.
